Q1. In-House SEO vs. Agency: Which Delivers Better ROI in 2027?

In 2027, the better ROI comes from whichever model ships revenue-focused content that AI engines cite. Most seed-to-Series-B and mid-market companies get there faster with a full-stack agency, because early SEO rarely fills 40 hours a week. One hire also cannot cover product, content, technical, and PR work alone. Enterprises with complex products and real engineering access usually win with an in-house team.

The Short Answer, by Company Type

The verdict depends on two inputs: how much SEO work you actually have, and how fast you need it shipped. Here is how the models line up.

Better ROI Model by Company Stage
Company stage Better ROI model Why
Pre-product-market fit Neither yet Prove conversion with paid channels first
Seed to Series B Full-stack agency Not enough work for a full-time hire
Mid-market Hybrid One internal owner, external execution
Enterprise In-house team Engineering access unlocks large revenue

⏰ Why workload decides it first

Eli Schwartz, who led SEO at SurveyMonkey, puts it bluntly. Early in a company’s organic journey, “there aren’t forty hours a week of work that can be done on SEO.” Most early work is strategy and research, and even execution may not be a full-time job.

A full-time hire with half a job’s worth of work still costs a full salary. That gap is where early-stage ROI quietly disappears.

What Changed About “ROI” Itself

For a decade, SEO ROI meant rankings, then traffic, then maybe leads. That chain is breaking. Ahrefs found that AI Overviews now cut the click-through rate for position one by 58%. A page can rank first and still lose most of its clicks.

At the same time, Semrush found the average visitor from AI search is worth 4.4 times a traditional organic visitor, based on conversion rates. Fewer clicks arrive, but the ones that do are closer to buying.

✅ The new question to ask

So the real question is not “in-house or agency?” It is “which model can get us cited in AI answers and turn that into pipeline?” Rankings still matter, because AI engines often pull from pages that already rank. But rankings are now the floor, not the finish line.

Q2. How Has AI Search Changed What You’re Actually Hiring For?

AI search changed the SEO job from ranking a page to becoming the answer. AI Overviews cut position-one click-through rate by 58% (Ahrefs, 2026), while AI search visitors convert about 4.4 times better (Semrush, 2025). Earning citations from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini requires prompt testing and citation tracking. MaximusLabs AI treats this as a data-science problem and tracks AI visibility daily across every major engine.

How SEO Hiring Used to Work

Picture the old job description: “Own keyword strategy, grow traffic, report monthly rankings.” The report showed rankings going up. Nobody asked where the pipeline was.

❌ What that model optimized for

Traditional SEO, in-house or agency, was built for Google’s blue links. Teams were hired for keyword research, on-page fixes, and link building. Those skills still matter, but they target a shrinking results page.

What Changed

Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Google AI Overviews. They get one answer with a handful of brands in it. A brand is either inside that answer or invisible. Ahrefs measured the damage on Google itself: AI Overviews now keep 58 of every 100 clicks a top page used to earn.

Semrush found AI search visitors convert at 4.4 times the rate of traditional organic visitors. Being mentioned is not the finish line, though. Turning that mention into pipeline is.

⚠️ Why this is a different skill

The Princeton-led GEO study gave this discipline its name. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is optimizing content so AI engines select and cite it. The researchers showed targeted changes can lift visibility in AI answers by up to 40%. Their best methods were adding citations, quotations, and statistics. Keyword stuffing offered little to no improvement.

That is a testing discipline, not a checklist, and few in-house teams have built the tooling.

💸 What GEO talent costs to hire

Posted pay for GEO and AEO roles in 2026 ranged from about $75,000 to $174,000. Nobody has priced the role consistently yet, so one hire is a real bet.

What MaximusLabs AI Does About It

MaximusLabs AI runs AEO as its flagship service, with SEO as the foundation, and does strategy and execution in one team. In practice, that means four systems working together:

  • Per-client intelligence graphs: AI agents that research the market and reason over each client’s own context
  • Daily AI-visibility tracking: prompts re-run across every major engine and regression-tested
  • A citation-intelligence engine: it maps which sources each engine trusts, then gets the client into them
  • The Two Pillars: Owned AEO, where your content becomes the answer, and Earned AEO, where reviews, Reddit, press, and listicles make AI trust you

Humans approve everything that ships.

📊 What the proof looks like

MaximusLabs’ own read is that GEO is not “SEO plus.” It is a data-science problem, because you have to know how each engine chooses sources before you can earn a place in its answer. I might be wrong on the details as engines change, but the results so far point one way. For Oliv AI, a B2B SaaS client, the outcome was “64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents (Oliv AI)”.

MaximusLabs AI measures that work on pipeline, not citations alone. For the same client, the result was “27 qualified leads, $47K+ pipeline, 26% close rate in 4 months (Oliv AI)”. Hold any hire or agency to that bar in 2027.

Q3. What Does an In-House SEO Team Really Cost Once Fully Loaded?

A Glassdoor-median SEO manager earns about $143,000 a year. Benefits make up roughly 30% of total employer compensation costs (BLS, 2025), pushing that manager to about $205,000 fully loaded. Add tools, writers, and engineering time, and a working in-house pod costs about $20,000 a month, or roughly $800 per content asset. Traditional agency retainers typically bundle talent and tools into one monthly fee.

Start With the Number Most Teams Skip

The most common mistake is comparing a base salary to an agency retainer. That is not a fair comparison. A salary is only the visible part of what an employee costs.

💰 The benefits multiplier

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks this precisely. For private industry, wages and salaries made up about 70% of employer compensation costs in December 2025, with benefits covering the rest. In plain terms, multiply salary by about 1.43 to get the real cost.

Apply that to Glassdoor’s 2026 median SEO manager salary of $143,456. The fully loaded cost lands near $205,000 a year, before a single tool or article.

Build the Full Stack of Costs

Here is what a working in-house SEO function actually needs:

  1. People: an SEO lead, plus writers and a share of an engineer’s sprints
  2. Benefits and payroll taxes: about 30% of total compensation
  3. Tools: rank tracking, crawlers, and now AI-visibility tracking
  4. Content production: writing, editing, design, and publishing
  5. Ramp time: months before a new hire ships meaningful work

📊 What it adds up to

MaximusLabs AI’s own cost comparison puts a working in-house pod at about $20,000 a month. That figure is conservative. The Glassdoor-median manager alone costs about $17,000 a month fully loaded, before writers or tools.

The same comparison puts the in-house cost at about $800 per content asset. A traditional agency lands near $260 per asset, because writers, tools, and editors are shared across clients.

The Hidden Costs That Never Show on a Spreadsheet

Two costs rarely appear in the budget line. The first is the empty seat. Hiring an experienced SEO lead can take months, and nothing ships while the role sits open.

The second is turnover. If your one SEO hire leaves, the knowledge, the roadmap, and the relationships with engineering leave too. You pay the recruiting and ramp costs all over again.

⚠️ The old math still holds

Phil Singleton made this point years before AI search existed. In 2016, an entry-level SEO specialist cost about $3,500 a month. For “the same amount or less,” he argued, a company could engage an experienced agency. Salaries have risen since, and the gap has widened.

What I Learned Running This From the Inside

Before founding MaximusLabs AI, I was the second employee at WiseMonk and headed go-to-market. SEO drove 98% of the company’s revenue. MaximusLabs’ founding view came from that seat: a lean team only works when every dollar goes to content that converts.

The cost question is never just “what does a hire cost?” It is “what does each shipped, revenue-driving asset cost?” That number is the one to compare.

MaximusLabs AI bundles strategy, an SEO manager, a designer, writers, and software into one engagement, so the full-stack cost is visible from day one. Its work starts with bottom-of-funnel content tied to the buyer profile, which is the cost per asset worth comparing. No hiring lag, no empty seat, and no separate tool bills.

Q4. What Does an Agency Retainer Really Buy, and Where Does It Leak?

An SEO agency retainer pays off only when the agency ships work that drives revenue. Many traditional agencies deliver long PDF audits full of low-impact fixes and pitch senior strategists who later hand accounts to juniors. The result is a repeating cycle: audit, small fixes, flat results, lost engineering trust, then a new agency. Most of these retainers also stop at Google rankings and ignore AI answers.

What the Retainer Promised

The monthly report arrives with charts, keyword movements, and “completed tasks.” The VP of Marketing forwards it to the founder. The founder replies with one line: “What did this do for pipeline?”

❌ How traditional retainers were built

Traditional agencies sold rankings, impressions, and clicks. Those are vanity metrics, numbers that look like progress without tying to revenue. Retainers were locked to a fixed scope. Strategy often came from one firm and execution from another, and content was written for blue links.

Where the Money Leaks

There are three common leaks, and most frustrated buyers have lived through at least one.

Where Agency Retainers Leak Value
Leak What it looks like What it costs you
The PDF audit A 50-page report of minor technical checks Months spent on fixes with no revenue impact
The senior-to-junior handover Senior people pitch, junior staff run the account Strategy quality drops after signing
Strategy without shipping Plans handed to your team to build Most recommendations never launch

⚠️ The SEO death spiral

Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite, describes the pattern clearly. An agency delivers “a 50 page PDF” with a huge roadmap, and “most of it’s zero impact work.” Results stay flat, the agency says to wait a year, and the client moves on. He calls it a “death spiral of zero impact work.”

Each loop also burns engineering trust, so the next agency starts with less goodwill.

What Changed in the AI Era

AI engines now answer the buyer’s question directly, with 5 to 10 brands named. AI recommends brands it trusts, because its own credibility rides on the recommendation. An audit that fixes alt tags does not earn that trust.

Earning a place in the answer takes citation testing and presence on the sources each engine relies on. Most traditional retainers were never scoped for that work.

💡 My read on “GEO as an add-on”

MaximusLabs’ read is that many agencies now sell GEO as an extra line item without understanding how large language models retrieve sources. My question to them is simple: how can you sell something as a service if you don’t understand it completely? Ask any agency to show its testing method before you sign.

Owned AEO makes the client’s content the answer, and Earned AEO builds trust through reviews, Reddit, press, and listicles.

✅ Where the retainer should land

The test of any retainer is what it does for pipeline. For Oliv AI, MaximusLabs’ work led to “30-40% of inbound now AI-sourced (Oliv AI).” That is the kind of line a monthly report should carry.

MaximusLabs AI measures every engagement on pipeline, not on mentions, and humans approve everything that ships. The retainer buys shipped work across Google and AI engines, not a PDF.

Q5. Which Model Reaches ROI Faster, and Why Does Speed Matter Most?

Execution speed decides SEO ROI more than expertise does. An in-house hire typically takes one to three months to recruit, then more months to ramp up. A traditional agency usually needs about two weeks to align and another month to start. Strategy-only engagements see roughly 20% of their recommendations launched. A full-stack partner that writes, designs, and deploys the work shortens time to revenue most.

What matters is how long until the first revenue-driving page goes live, and how much of the plan ever ships.

⏰ Where the in-house clock goes

The in-house timeline hides two delays. First comes the open seat, often one to three months. Then the new hire spends weeks learning the product, the buyer, and who controls engineering sprints.

Nothing ships during either delay, but salary and benefits keep running.

Why Launch Rate Beats Expertise

Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite, learned this running a consulting practice. He would build a full strategy, and “maybe 20% of that was launched.” The rest stalled in the client’s queue.

So Graphite went full-stack. In his words, “it’s actually better and faster if we do everything ourselves.”

⚠️ The 20% problem

A plan that launches at 20% delivers a fifth of its value. That is why launch rate, the share of recommendations that actually go live, belongs in every vendor comparison.

Before signing, ask who publishes the pages. If the answer is “your team,” budget for the queue.

Speed Over Clever Features

Teams often debate tools instead of shipping. Ethan Smith disagrees: what matters most, he says, is “velocity” rather than a CMS feature that formats pages for AI engines. Graphite built a whole Webflow team because it was simply fast.

✅ What this means for AI search

Speed matters even more now. AI engines answer with a handful of brands, and those slots go to sources that publish, earn citations, and keep content fresh. A slow team lets a competitor become the default answer.

My Read: Run Fast, Then Steer

MaximusLabs AI’s read is that most teams over-plan and under-ship. I think about it like a toy dash car. It runs straight until it hits a wall, turns, and keeps going. You learn more from ten shipped pages than from one perfect roadmap.

I might be overstating it for regulated industries, where legal review sets the pace. Even there, shorten the path from approved idea to live page.

MaximusLabs AI onboards new clients in two days and runs strategy and execution in one team, so recommendations do not wait in a client’s backlog. Humans approve everything that ships, and every engagement is measured on pipeline, not on mentions. That combination is what turns speed into revenue rather than activity.

Q6. Why Do Single In-House SEO Hires So Often Fail?

Lone in-house SEO hires fail because job descriptions ask one person to master content, project management, engineering, and analytics. Few people have all four skills. Placed inside Marketing without engineering sprints, the hire gets blocked on technical changes and stalls. Companies that build in-house succeed more often when SEO reports into Product, with engineering and data-science time already allocated.

The Job Description Nobody Can Fill

Picture the posting. “Senior SEO Manager: own content strategy, manage projects, partner with engineering, and build analytics dashboards.” The hiring manager wants one person. The market rarely has that person.

❌ Four jobs in one seat

Eli Schwartz, who ran SEO at SurveyMonkey, has flagged this pattern in SEO job postings. Many ask for someone who understands content, project management, engineering, and analytics. “Finding a person with all these divergent skills,” he wrote, “might be an impossible mission.”

So companies hire someone strong in one or two areas. The other two quietly go undone.

Blocked on Day One

Schwartz lived the engineering problem himself. On his first day at SurveyMonkey, he asked for a routine technical change. An engineer refused, with a finality that suggested no future requests would get through either.

He did not give up. He built the relationship over time. By the time that engineer left, he had become an SEO advocate, and together they drove “hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue” from SEO changes.

⚠️ The marketing silo

That story ended well because Schwartz knew how to win engineering over. Most lone hires do not get that chance. When SEO sits in Marketing with no reserved engineering time, every technical fix becomes a favor.

The hire writes tickets, waits, and reports flat results. Leadership blames the hire, not the structure.

What AI Search Adds to the Load

The job has also grown. AI engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini now pick a few trusted sources per answer. Earning a spot takes citation testing, structured data, and presence on review sites and Reddit.

That is a fifth discipline stacked onto an already impossible role. One person cannot test prompts daily while also fixing site speed and editing articles.

💡 A lesson from engineering school

I studied mechanical engineering before I worked in growth. One rule stuck with me: a machine built to do four jobs usually does all four badly. SEO teams follow the same physics.

MaximusLabs AI’s view follows from this. Brands competing for AI citations need separate owners for content, technical work, and off-site trust, all coordinated toward pipeline.

The Fix If You Build In-House

If you hire internally, change the structure before you change the person:

  1. Report SEO into Product, not only Marketing
  2. Reserve engineering sprints for SEO work each quarter
  3. Give data-science access for measurement and testing
  4. Fund content design so pages ship without borrowing writers

✅ When the hire can actually succeed

With those four pieces in place, a strong in-house lead can deliver. Without them, even the best candidate stalls.

MaximusLabs AI covers all four disciplines in one team, so no single person carries content, technical, analytics, and off-site work alone. Its Owned AEO and Earned AEO pillars split the work between making content the answer and building trust across reviews, Reddit, and press. For Oliv AI, that produced “64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents (Oliv AI)”.

Q7. When Does In-House Win, When Does an Agency Win, and When Should You Wait?

The right model depends on company stage and real SEO workload. Before product-market fit, most companies should wait and use paid channels to prove conversion. From seed to Series B, an agency usually wins on full-stack speed. Mid-market companies often suit a hybrid. Enterprises with complex codebases, compliance needs, and large organic footprints usually win in-house, where cross-functional access unlocks revenue.

Stage One: Before Product-Market Fit

Ethan Smith’s advice to early startups surprises people. When they ask for SEO help, his first response is “don’t do it at all.” New domains lack the authority to rank, so effort spent early compounds slowly.

💸 Rent first, then buy

Eli Schwartz frames this with a housing analogy. Paid marketing “is like renting a house” with no equity, while SEO “is like owning a house.” Renting makes sense while you still test which message converts.

So run paid ads first. Learn which pages, offers, and words turn visitors into pipeline. Then move budget into SEO with that data in hand.

Stage Two: Seed to Series B

This is where the 40-hour test matters most. Schwartz notes that early SEO rarely fills forty hours a week. A full-time hire with half a job still costs a full salary.

A full-stack agency fits this stage. It brings content, technical, and off-site skills without four hires.

⚠️ The honest disagreement

Experts split on this. Phil Singleton argues companies “will achieve greater success with a proven SEO agency.” Schwartz counters that growing companies eventually need deep internal integration that outside teams cannot drive alone. Both are right, just at different stages.

Stage Three: Mid-Market

Mid-market companies often have enough work for one internal owner but not a full team. That owner sets strategy, approves positioning, and unblocks engineering. An external partner handles production and testing.

The trigger to move here is usually a second product line or a sales team asking for more pages than one person can ship. This split is the hybrid model, covered in the next section.

Stage Four: Enterprise

In-house wins at scale. Complex codebases, strict compliance, and huge page counts reward people who sit inside the company. Search Engine Land’s enterprise estimates show agency costs can exceed in-house costs at the high end.

⭐ Large accounts need a named human

Even enterprises that keep agencies need dedicated people. Ethan Smith puts it simply: “the whales need a person to talk to.” If you hire an agency at this stage, insist on a named senior lead.

My Read on Fit

MaximusLabs AI’s read is that honesty about fit saves everyone money. MaximusLabs is not for companies wanting impact within a week, or teams choosing purely on price. I would rather say no early than sell a retainer that cannot work.

MaximusLabs AI works mainly with B2B SaaS, then healthcare and financial services companies, where AI search is reshaping how buyers shortlist vendors. It is built for growth-stage companies, where full-stack execution beats a lone hire. Every engagement is measured on pipeline, which keeps the fit question honest after signing.

Q8. How Should a Hybrid Model Split the Work?

A working hybrid keeps strategy, brand voice, buyer knowledge, and engineering unblocking inside the company. Content production, GEO testing, technical sprints, and off-page authority run with an external partner. One internal owner approves the work, and the partner ships it. When no internal owner exists, the partner’s work sits in a queue and the hybrid fails.

The Internal Owner’s Job

The hybrid lives or dies on one person. This owner does not write every article. They make decisions fast and clear the path.

✅ What stays inside

  • Positioning and brand voice: how the product is described and why it wins
  • Buyer knowledge: the ideal customer profile, objections, and sales-call insights
  • Engineering access: sprint time for technical fixes and schema changes
  • Final approval: a fast yes or no on every page before it ships

The agency drafts, and your team approves and corrects. Never the other way around.

Mapping the Four Disciplines

SEO work splits cleanly across four disciplines: Product, Content, Technical, and PR (off-page authority). A hybrid assigns each one an owner.

Hybrid SEO Model: Who Owns Each Discipline

💡 How the founder’s voice survives outsourcing

The usual fear is that outsourced content sounds generic. MaximusLabs AI handles this with a founder’s voice process. We sit with the leadership team, learn the vision and the right to win, and capture the founder’s view of the market. Then every article reflects that view, and the client approves it.

How Hybrids Fail

Hybrids break in predictable ways:

  1. No owner: drafts pile up with nobody empowered to approve them
  2. Too many owners: five reviewers rewrite every page into mush
  3. No engineering time: technical fixes wait for a quarter that never comes
  4. Split goals: the inside team tracks traffic while the partner tracks citations

⚠️ One scoreboard, not two

The fix for split goals is one shared scoreboard. Both sides should report on pipeline from organic and AI search. Rankings and citations are inputs, not the result.

What This Looks Like in AI Search

AI engines reward consistency across your site and the wider web. Your pages, reviews, Reddit threads, and press mentions must tell the same story. A hybrid with one owner keeps that story straight.

⭐ My read on the split

Q10. How Do You Vet an Agency or an In-House Hire Before Committing?

To vet an SEO agency, call at least five live client references. Check their Ahrefs or Semrush traffic curves from each client’s onboarding month. Ask for proof of shipped work and a documented method for testing AI citations. To vet an in-house hire, run four stages: recruiter screen, hiring manager interview, cross-functional panel, and executive sign-off. The panel covers product, engineering, content, and data.

Step 1: Check References the Hard Way

Sales decks always look good. References tell you what happens after the contract is signed.

✅ The five-reference rule

  1. Ask for five live clients, not past ones or hand-picked case studies
  2. Call them yourself, without the agency on the line
  3. Ask who runs the account day to day, and whether that changed after signing
  4. Ask what shipped, in pages, fixes, and placements, not reports
  5. Ask about pipeline, not just traffic or rankings

If an agency cannot produce five current clients willing to talk, treat that as data.

Step 2: Read the Traffic Curve Yourself

Do not rely on screenshots in a pitch deck. Look up each reference client’s domain in Ahrefs or Semrush, and find the month the agency started.

📊 What a healthy curve looks like

  • Good sign: steady growth that begins a few months after onboarding
  • Warning sign: a spike followed by a sharp drop, which can signal shortcuts
  • Neutral sign: flat traffic with rising conversions, which can be fine in AI search

Remember that AI Overviews now take a large share of clicks. A flat traffic line no longer proves failure, so ask for pipeline data too.

Step 3: Test for Shipping and AI Search Depth

Ask every agency who publishes the pages. Ethan Smith found strategy-only engagements launched only about 20% of their plans. You want a partner that writes, designs, and deploys.

⚠️ Questions that expose surface-level GEO

  • How do you test whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini cites a client?
  • How often do you re-run those tests?
  • Which off-site sources, such as reviews, Reddit, and listicles, do you work on?
  • Can you show a client’s citation share before and after your work?

Vague answers usually mean GEO is a slide, not a practice.
