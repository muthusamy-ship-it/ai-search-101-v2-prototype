Q1. Why does ranking on Gemini now mean becoming the answer, not holding a position?

Gemini returns one synthesized answer with a handful of cited sources, not ten blue links. Roughly 60% of searches now end without a click, while AI search visitors convert at about 4.4x the value of traditional organic visitors (Semrush, 2025). The goal moves from occupying a position to being the source the model cites, because the shortlist it names becomes the buyer's entire consideration set.

📉 The report that stopped making sense

A Head of Organic Growth I spoke with last quarter had a clean dashboard. Rankings up, impressions up, three new featured snippets. Her CEO asked one question in the Monday review: where is the pipeline?

Nothing in the rank report could answer it. The keywords held. The sessions did not.

🧾 What the old playbook was actually selling

For fifteen years, agencies sold position, impressions, and clicks on fixed-scope retainers. Strategy came from one firm, execution from another, and the content was written for a page of ten links.

That worked while the ten links existed. The reporting line ran from rank to traffic to pipeline, and everyone accepted the middle step on faith. Vanity metrics survived because the funnel was long enough to hide them, which is the core of the difference between GEO and traditional SEO.

🔀 What changed when the answer became singular

Buyers now ask Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews. They get one answer naming five to ten brands, and that list is the evaluation set.

Krishna Kaanth puts the founder version of this anxiety plainly:

"Traffic is going down. Over 70% are zero-click searches. Considering agents are coming, if they're not coming to my website, AI can just read my content, answer the question, and reroute the traffic to some other player who's paying them."

The second half matters more than the first. If your company is not on that list, you are not in the buying conversation at all. There is no page two to recover on.

💰 Fewer sessions, better sessions

Here is the inversion that reframes the whole channel. AI search sends less traffic and more intent, at roughly 4.4x the value per visitor (Semrush, 2025).

The buyer arrives having already asked the engine six questions. The engine named you. They are pre-sold, which is why a 40% traffic drop can sit alongside a pipeline increase, a pattern we track in our work on the decline in search referral traffic.

MaximusLabs AI measures every engagement on pipeline, not on mentions, and reports citation share against revenue rather than impressions. We start at bottom-of-funnel content, because the engines already answer "what is X" without anyone's help. Traditional agencies still ship the rank report that Gemini stopped honoring.

🎯 The KPI swap to make this week

Being cited is the means. Pipeline is the outcome.

Replace one line in your next report. Where it says average position, put citation share across the prompts your ideal customer actually types, then tag the sessions those answers send. My read is that most teams discover two things at once: they are cited less than they assumed, and the citations they do hold convert better than anything else they own.

That is the shift this guide is built on. Ranking is the floor. Becoming the answer is the job, which is exactly what Answer Engine Optimization is built to do.

Q2. How does Gemini actually decide which pages to cite?

Gemini does not rank pages. It gates, fans out, retrieves, and scores passages. A learned prediction score decides whether a query deserves live Google grounding, defaulting to 0.3 in the Gemini API and 0.7 in Vertex AI Search. Google then expands the query into multiple sub-queries through fan-out. Only passages at a confidence score of 0.6 or above enter the grounded answer, with citationThreshold defaulting to 0.6.

🚪 The gate before the search

Most SEO teams picture Gemini searching Google for every prompt. It does not. A classifier predicts how much live grounding would improve the answer, and only then calls Search.

Google's own documentation uses two clear examples. "Who won the latest F1 grand prix?" scores around 0.97. "Write a poem about peonies" scores around 0.13. The second never touches your content, no matter how good it is.

📊 The numbers that govern inclusion

Gemini Retrieval and Citation Thresholds
Control Default What it decides
Dynamic retrieval threshold (Gemini API) 0.3 Whether the query triggers live grounding
Dynamic retrieval threshold (Vertex AI Search) 0.7 Same decision, stricter setting
Chunk confidence score 0.6 Whether a retrieved passage can enter the answer
citationThreshold 0.6 Whether that passage gets an attributed citation
antiCitationThreshold 0.8 How aggressively weak attributions are suppressed

Read that table once and a lot of advice falls away. Nothing in the pipeline scores your page. Everything scores a passage.

✂️ Why page-level thinking fails here

Query fan-out compounds this. Google expands one prompt into several related sub-queries, then retrieves separately against each, as described in Google Search Central's generative AI optimization guide. You can map those expansions with our query fan-out generator.

So a single buying question can pull three different passages from three different sites. Your 3,000 word guide competes paragraph by paragraph, not URL by URL. A page can be indexed, relevant, and still lose because no 80 word block inside it answers a sub-query cleanly.

MaximusLabs AI writes every section as a standalone 40 to 80 word answer block, scored before publication on a ten dimension rubric that fails any section whose opening cannot survive extraction. We built that rule because the scored unit is the passage. It is a dull discipline, and it is the one that moves citation share.

🔍 The audit to run on Monday

Take your highest intent page. Read only the first three sentences under each heading, with the heading removed.

Ask whether each block still answers something a buyer would ask. Then ask whether it contains a number, a date, or a named source. Blocks that pass both tests are candidates for grounding. Blocks that fail are decoration.

🧠 What I am still unsure about

Published thresholds come from the Gemini API and Vertex AI Search documentation. Google has not confirmed that consumer Search grounding uses the same defaults, and I suspect it does not.

I treat the documented numbers as the best available model of the mechanism, not as the live settings. MaximusLabs AI tests against real prompts rather than API endpoints for exactly that reason, which matters later when we get to measurement.

Q3. What has to be true before Gemini can cite you at all?

Three conditions come straight from Google's documentation on generative AI features: the page must be indexed, snippet eligible, and included in Search's generative AI features. Blocking snippets or AI features removes the page regardless of content quality. Speed compounds the problem, since fast rendering pages average 6.7 AI citations against 2.1 for slower ones. MaximusLabs AI audits these three gates in week one of every engagement, before any content is commissioned.

🚧 The three gates, in order

Google states that generative AI features in Search are rooted in core Search ranking, which means the index is the entry point. No index, no grounding.

Snippet eligibility is the second gate. If Google cannot show a text preview of your page, it has nothing to lift into an answer.

The third gate is inclusion in generative AI features specifically. These are separate controls, and teams regularly pass two and fail the third, which a technical SEO and website audit surfaces quickly.

⚠️ The directives that quietly disqualify you

Directives That Block Gemini Visibility
Signal Effect on Gemini visibility Action
noindex Page cannot be retrieved at all ✅ Remove on revenue pages
nosnippet No text available to extract ✅ Remove, then re-request indexing
max-snippet:0 Functionally the same as nosnippet ✅ Set generously or omit
data-nosnippet on key blocks Your answer block is excluded ⚠️ Audit templates, not just pages
Blocked AI feature controls Removal from generative surfaces ⚠️ Decide deliberately, not by default

Most of these were set years ago by someone protecting content from scrapers. Nobody revisited them when the answer surface arrived.

⏰ Rendering speed and JavaScript

If your main content loads through client side JavaScript, assume some of it never reaches the grounding step. Critical text belongs in the initial HTML, which you can verify with our AI crawlability checker.

The citation gap between fast and slow pages, 6.7 versus 2.1 on average, is large enough to change a quarter. I would not treat that figure as causal on its own, because fast sites tend to be well maintained sites. Both halves point the same direction anyway.

✅ The 20 page eligibility audit

  1. List your 20 highest intent URLs, the ones you would defend in a board meeting.
  2. Confirm index status for each in Google Search Console's index coverage report.
  3. Check the rendered HTML for nosnippet, max-snippet, and data-nosnippet in templates.
  4. Confirm AI crawler access in robots.txt, including GPTBot and PerplexityBot for other engines.
  5. View each page with JavaScript disabled and note what disappears.
  6. Record pass or fail per URL, then fix fails before writing anything new.

That sheet takes an afternoon. It has saved clients a quarter of wasted content budget more than once.

🧱 Foundation first, not foundation only

Krishna's framing fits here better than mine:

"SEO is the foundation floor. GEO is the building on top."

Technical SEO became the entry fee, not the strategy. MaximusLabs AI runs Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) as the flagship work, with technical SEO underneath it as the week one sprint, covering crawler access, JavaScript minimization, and clean semantic markup. Content only GEO retainers skip this step, then wonder why nothing gets cited.

Q4. Does ranking #1 on Google still guarantee a Gemini citation?

No. After Gemini 3 shipped as the default AI Overviews model, reliance on top ten organic results fell from roughly 76% to 38%, with about 31% of AI Overview citations coming from positions beyond 100. Gemini re-ranks and blends results before grounding, so a well structured page at position 15 can beat a badly formatted page at position 1. Princeton's GEO study measured a 115% visibility lift for a position five page that added citations.

💥 The decoupling, in two numbers

Rank Reliance Before and After Gemini 3
What you report Before Gemini 3 After Gemini 3
Share of AI citations from top ten organic ~76% ~38%
Citations from beyond position 100 Marginal ~31%
Best predictor of citation Rank position Structure and sourcing quality

Rank still helps. It simply stopped being the deciding input, because the re-ranking happens after retrieval and before generation. Our breakdown of Gemini's impact on AI search tracks the full shift.

The Erlin Gemini SEO study states the practical version of this cleanly: "Lower-ranked pages with superior structure are cited over top-ranked pages with poor format."

⚖️ The equalizer effect, and why challengers should care

This is the most useful finding in the Princeton GEO paper, and almost nobody uses it as an argument. Across roughly 10,000 queries and nine datasets, adding citations lifted a position five page's visibility by 115.1%.

The same study measured what else moves the needle:

  • ✅ Adding statistics: up to 41% improvement in position adjusted word count
  • ✅ Adding quotations from credible sources: around 28% improvement
  • ❌ Keyword stuffing: roughly 10% below baseline, worse on some engines

Read as a budget decision, that is striking. The lifts come from evidence, not from optimization volume. A challenger with better sourcing can displace an incumbent without ever outranking it, which is the premise behind GEO for SaaS startups.

🧮 What this changes in reporting

MaximusLabs AI reports citation share rather than average position, because the two stopped tracking each other. In practice we group pages by structural quality instead of rank band, which surfaces a pattern clients rarely expect: their best ranked page is often their least citable one.

MaximusLabs AI's data points one way here, and the published decoupling numbers point the same way, though both are measured on AI Overviews rather than Gemini chat. I might be reading the chat surface too confidently from Overviews data. Our approach to GEO measurement and metrics keeps those surfaces separate for that reason.

🔁 The audit that replaces the rank check

Pull your top ten pages by rank. Score each on four things only: does a direct answer appear in the first 30% of the body, does every section carry a verifiable number, are comparisons in tables, and are processes in numbered lists?

Then compare that score against citation share. The mismatch is your roadmap. On most sites, two or three strong pages carry the citations while the rank leaders sit unquoted.

🧷 Where the old report still earns its place

I am not going to tell you rank is dead, because that is lazy and the index is still the entry point. Rank gets you retrievable. Structure and evidence get you cited.

MaximusLabs AI spends client budget on sourcing, structure, and earned trust signals rather than on chasing position one, and measures the result as pipeline influence. Proof from one B2B SaaS engagement: 64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents, documented in the Oliv AI case study.

Q5. What changed overnight when Gemini 3 became the default AI Overviews model?

On 27 January 2026, Gemini 3 became the default AI Overviews model worldwide. Over 84,000 domains were affected. Of previously cited domains, 42.4% dropped out, while 51.7% of the new citation pool was new domains. Average sources per answer rose from 11.55 to 15.22, a 31.8% increase. Of the 500 most cited domains, only one disappeared.

📅 What the Tuesday dashboards showed

A marketing manager at a mid market SaaS company messaged me that week. Her AI Overview citations had vanished from eleven pages, and nothing on the site had changed.

Nothing on her site had to change. Google changed the model underneath the answer. Her rank report was identical, which is what made the whole thing so disorienting. Our running log of Google AI platform updates tracks each of these swaps.

🔀 The part nobody expected

The volume of citations went up, not down. Answers started pulling 15.22 sources on average instead of 11.55.

More slots, and yet 42.4% of the previously cited domains lost theirs. The pool widened and the occupants were swapped at the same time.

⚠️ The asymmetry is the real lesson

Here is the finding worth pinning to a wall. Of the 500 most cited domains, only one dropped out. Everything below that line churned, and market concentration rose 44%.

Citation Impact by Domain Tier, 27 January 2026
Tier What happened on 27 January 2026
Top 500 cited domains ✅ Almost entirely untouched, one exit
Mid tier domains ⚠️ Heavy replacement inside a wider source pool
Long tail domains ❌ 42.4% dropped, 51.7% of new pool was new entrants

Gemini 3 protected established authorities and churned the long tail. Model updates are not random when read that way. They punish thin presence and reward accumulated trust, a pattern our analysis of Gemini's impact on AI search breaks down in full.

🧱 Why volume strategies broke first

Sites with fifty thin pages had fifty fragile citations. Sites with five genuinely authoritative pages mostly held.

I keep coming back to a parallel from index investing. Breadth without quality gets you average exposure to volatility, not protection from it. MaximusLabs AI treats brand and entity authority as the volatility hedge, which is why our engagements front load depth over publishing volume.

💰 What this means for the next model swap

There will be another one. Treat citation share as a position you have to earn into the protected tier, not a score you maintain.

MaximusLabs AI runs Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) with SEO underneath it, as strategy and execution in one team, measured on pipeline rather than mentions. Our citation intelligence engine maps which sources each engine actually trusts, then we work the client into those sources. Daily AI visibility tracking across engines tells us when a reshuffle hits, not a month later.

Proof from a B2B SaaS engagement: 64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents (Oliv AI). That position is the kind that survives a model swap, and it produced 27 qualified leads, $47K+ pipeline, 26% close rate in 4 months (Oliv AI).

⏰ The move for this quarter

Pick your five highest intent pages and make each one defensible: original data, named sources, visible author credentials, and earned mentions pointing at it.

Then stop publishing the next twenty thin pages. My honest read is that most teams cannot tell the difference between the two strategies until a model update does the sorting for them.

Q6. How should you structure a page so Gemini can extract it?

Front load every section. A 30 to 60 word direct answer placed immediately after a question style heading, inside the top 30% of body text, sits in the zone that produces a 44.2% citation rate. Comparison tables carry 1.8x citation likelihood, and step by step lists 2.1x. MaximusLabs AI enforces a 40 to 80 word standalone answer block on every H2 and scores it before publication on a ten dimension rubric.

✂️ The habit that costs you citations

Most B2B pages warm up. Two paragraphs of context, a nod to why the topic matters, then the answer in paragraph three.

Human readers tolerate that. The retrieval step does not reward it, because citations cluster in the first third of the body in a ski ramp shape. Our guide to AEO answer structure covers the placement rules in detail.

📝 One rewrite, before and after

Take a heading like "How long does AEO take to show results?"

❌ Before: "Timelines vary by industry, competition, and content maturity. Many factors influence how quickly brands see movement, and no two programs are alike. In our experience, patience matters."

✅ After: "Most B2B SaaS programs see first AI citations in 60 to 90 days, with compounding gains after month four. Oliv AI reached 27 qualified leads, $47K+ pipeline, 26% close rate in 4 months. Timelines stretch in crowded categories with weak existing authority."

Same word count. The second version answers the heading on its own, carries a number, and survives extraction with nothing around it.

📊 The structural multipliers worth building into templates

Measured Structural Multipliers for AI Citations
Format Measured effect Use it for
Answer in top 30% of body 44.2% citation rate Every question style heading
Comparison table 1.8x citation likelihood Vendor, plan, or approach comparisons
Step by step numbered list 2.1x citation likelihood Setup, audit, and process answers
Added statistics Up to 41% visibility improvement Any claim you can source
Added quotations from credible sources Around 28% improvement Expert and platform statements
Keyword stuffing ❌ Roughly 10% below baseline Nothing

The Princeton GEO study measured those last three across roughly 10,000 queries and nine datasets. Evidence beats optimization volume, which is the opposite of how most content briefs are written.

⚙️ The template MaximusLabs AI actually ships

MaximusLabs AI rebuilt its editorial template around extractability. Every H2 opens with a standalone answer block, then expands with analysis, then carries at least one primary source, then adds a practitioner judgment. The full method sits in our GEO content optimization workflow.

Every comparison becomes a table, and every process becomes a numbered list. Nothing publishes below 70 out of 100 on our rubric, and 80 for pillar pages. Readability sits at a hard floor too, because dense prose scores badly and reads worse.

✅ The six point extractability check

  1. Does a direct answer appear in the first three sentences under the heading?
  2. Would that block still make sense with the heading removed?
  3. Does it contain a number, a date, or a named source?
  4. Are comparisons in a table rather than prose?
  5. Are processes in numbered steps?
  6. Is every sentence under 25 words?

Run it on one money page this week, ideally with our AI content optimizer. Most teams find two sections that pass and six that do not.

MaximusLabs AI measures the result in citation share and pipeline, not in word count shipped. Traditional agencies optimize the title tag. We optimize the paragraph a model can lift.

Q7. Which popular Gemini tactics are myths you should drop?

Google Search ignores llms.txt files, requires no special content chunking, and does not require structured data for generative AI features. A Search Atlas and Quoleady analysis across OpenAI, Gemini, and Perplexity found no correlation between schema density and citation rates. Google retired FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026. Reddit sits near 0.1% of Gemini chat citations versus about 4.01% in AI Overviews.

🧾 The myth table

Gemini Tactic Myths Versus Primary Sources
Popular claim What the primary source says Verdict
"Publish llms.txt to get into Gemini" Google Search ignores llms.txt and similar AI text files ❌ No effect on Google
"Chunk your content for the LLM" No chunking requirement stated in Google's guidance ❌ Not a requirement
"Schema is a citation lever" No correlation between schema density and citation rate across three engines ❌ Not a lever
"FAQ schema wins rich results" FAQ rich results retired 7 May 2026 ❌ Retired
"More keywords, more AI visibility" Keyword stuffing scored roughly 10% below baseline ❌ Actively harmful
"Post on Reddit to win Gemini chat" Reddit near 0.1% in chat, about 4.01% in AI Overviews ⚠️ Right surface, wrong engine

⚠️ Where schema still earns its keep

Dropping schema entirely would be the wrong read. Structured data remains parsing hygiene and a second order entity signal, which means it helps machines resolve who you are. Our schema markup basics guide draws that line explicitly.

MaximusLabs AI ships Article markup, Person markup with credentials and knowsAbout, and Organization markup linked to Wikidata, as technical hygiene rather than as a citation play. When a prospect asks why our proposal carries less schema work than a competitor's, that distinction is the answer.

Schema may still matter indirectly through entity resolution, even where direct citation correlation is null. I hold that as a hypothesis, not a finding.

📝 What to build instead of FAQ schema

The retirement of FAQ rich results removed the SERP reward, not the reader value. Visible on page question and answer blocks still get extracted, because the passage is what gets scored.

So keep the questions and lose the dependency. MaximusLabs AI writes those blocks as standalone answers inside the body, which is the same discipline that drives citation share anyway.

🧮 The budget reallocation this implies

Add up what your current plan spends on llms.txt setup, schema expansion, and keyword density tooling. On most proposals I review, that is a meaningful share of the technical line item.

Move it to three things instead: original data you can cite, credentialed authorship, and earned mentions on the surfaces Gemini actually grounds on.

⏰ The Monday version

Delete llms.txt tasks from the roadmap if Google is your target engine. Keep the file if you care about other platforms, and expect nothing from it here.

Then re-audit one page for the real levers: answer placement, a verifiable number, a named source, and visible author credentials. Our list of common GEO mistakes to avoid covers the rest.

MaximusLabs AI publishes what fails as readily as what works, because trust is the product in AEO. Our read is that the category sells technical theater because it is easy to invoice and hard to disprove.

Q8. Where off your own site does Gemini find its corroboration?

Mostly YouTube, review platforms, and communities. YouTube is consistently the top cited domain in Google AI Overviews and Gemini, cited both as video thumbnails and as text pulled from transcripts, with video presence correlating 0.737 with AI Overview citations. Reddit supplies about 4.01% of AI Overview citations. G2, Capterra, and analyst pages supply the corroboration that decides competitive, commercial prompts.

🎥 The channel most GEO programs skip entirely

Ethan Smith of Graphite described embedding a targeted video, "How to secure generated code," on a B2B application security page.

The page then lifted across ChatGPT, Gemini, YouTube search, and Google AI Overviews at the same time. One asset, four surfaces, one feedback loop.

📄 Why the transcript matters more than the video

Gemini cites text from transcripts, not just thumbnails. An untranscribed video is close to invisible to the grounding step.

So the asset is really the transcript, the description, and the chapters. The video is the delivery vehicle, and our multimodal search optimization guide covers how to prepare each element.

⭐ The corroboration layer buyers trigger

Commercial prompts behave differently from informational ones. When someone asks for the best tool in a category, the engine leans on third party pages it did not get from you.

🔁 Owned plus earned, not owned alone

MaximusLabs AI runs AEO on two pillars. Owned AEO makes your content the answer, and Earned AEO makes reviews, Reddit, press, and listicles give the engine a reason to trust you.

Most agencies only operate the first pillar, because it fits a content retainer. We identify the threads, profiles, and listicles already being cited for a client's buying questions, then earn presence inside those sources, which is the core of Reddit and forum AEO.

✅ The surface priority checklist

  1. Record three product demos answering real buyer questions, not feature tours.
  2. Transcribe every video and publish the transcript as crawlable text.
  3. Add timestamped chapters in the description field.
  4. Embed each video on the exact page that targets the matching query.

Questions, answered

7 questions
How does Gemini decide which pages to cite?

Gemini does not rank pages the way classic Search does. It gates, fans out, retrieves, then scores individual passages. • The gate: a learned prediction score decides whether a query benefits from live Google grounding. It defaults to 0.3 in the Gemini API and 0.7 in Vertex AI Search. • Query fan-out: Google expands one prompt into several sub-queries, then retrieves separately against each. • Passage scoring: only chunks at a confidence score of 0.6 or above enter the grounded answer, with citationThreshold at 0.6 and antiCitationThreshold at 0.8. The practical consequence is that your 3,000 word guide competes paragraph by paragraph, not URL by URL. A page can be indexed, relevant, and still lose because no single block inside it answers a sub-query cleanly. MaximusLabs AI writes every section as a standalone 40 to 80 word answer block and fails any section whose opening cannot survive extraction with nothing around it. That rule exists because the scored unit is the passage. Our GEO content optimization workflow applies it to every page we ship.

Does ranking first on Google guarantee a citation in Gemini?

No. The two metrics decoupled after Gemini 3 became the default AI Overviews model. • Reliance on top ten organic results fell from roughly 76% to 38%. • About 31% of AI Overview citations now come from organic positions beyond 100. • Gemini re-ranks and blends results before grounding, so structure can beat position. Princeton's GEO study quantified the upside for challengers. Across roughly 10,000 queries and nine datasets, adding citations lifted a position five page's visibility by 115.1%. Adding statistics improved visibility by up to 41%, quotations by around 28%, while keyword stuffing scored roughly 10% below baseline. Read that as a budget decision. The lifts come from evidence quality, not from optimization volume, which means a well sourced challenger can displace an incumbent without outranking it. MaximusLabs AI reports citation share instead of average position, and groups pages by structural quality rather than rank band. The pattern clients rarely expect is that their best ranked page is often their least citable one. Our comparison of GEO and traditional SEO covers why rank stays necessary but no longer sufficient.

What has to be true before Gemini can cite my site at all?

Three conditions, all from Google's own documentation on generative AI features: • The page must be indexed in Google Search. • The page must be snippet eligible, so Google can show a text preview. • The page must be included in Search's generative AI features, which is a separate control. Teams regularly pass two of the three and fail the third. The usual culprits are directives set years ago to deter scrapers: noindex, nosnippet, max-snippet:0, and data-nosnippet applied inside page templates rather than on individual URLs. Rendering compounds the problem. If your main content loads through client side JavaScript, assume some of it never reaches the grounding step, and that critical text belongs in the initial HTML. Fast rendering pages average 6.7 AI citations against 2.1 for slower ones. MaximusLabs AI audits these three gates in week one of every engagement, before any content gets commissioned, because content-only retainers skip this step and then wonder why nothing gets cited. Start with a technical SEO and website audit across your twenty highest intent URLs.

Do I need llms.txt, chunking, or FAQ schema to rank on Gemini?

No on all three, at least for Google specifically. • llms.txt: Google Search ignores llms.txt and similar AI text files. Maintaining one neither helps nor harms Google visibility, though other platforms may read it. • Chunking: Google states no special content chunking requirement. • FAQ schema: Google retired FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026, so FAQ markup no longer grants SERP eligibility. Schema more broadly is contested ground. A Search Atlas and Quoleady analysis across OpenAI, Gemini, and Perplexity found no correlation between schema density and citation rates. Domains with heavy JSON-LD were cited no more than domains with none, because authority and relevance drove selection. Structured data still earns its keep as parsing hygiene and second order entity disambiguation. MaximusLabs AI ships Article markup, Person markup with credentials, and Organization markup linked to Wikidata as technical hygiene, and never sells it as a citation lever. Keep visible on-page question and answer blocks instead of depending on FAQ markup, since the passage is what gets scored. Our schema markup guide draws that line explicitly.

Why does Gemini cite YouTube and review sites so heavily?

Because Gemini grounds on corroboration it did not get from you, and video is its most under-priced source. • YouTube is consistently the top cited domain in Google AI Overviews and Gemini, appearing both as video thumbnails and as text pulled from transcripts. • Video presence correlates 0.737 with AI Overview citations. • Reddit supplies about 4.01% of AI Overview citations, though it sits near 0.1% in Gemini chat, a roughly nine-fold surface gap. • G2, Capterra, and analyst pages decide competitive, commercial prompts. The transcript is the real asset, not the video. An untranscribed demo is close to invisible to the grounding step, so publish the transcript as crawlable text and add timestamped chapters in the description.
