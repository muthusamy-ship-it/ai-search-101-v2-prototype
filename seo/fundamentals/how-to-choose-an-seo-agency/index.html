A VP of Marketing opens the agency’s monthly report on a Tuesday morning. Rankings are up on 40 keywords, and the chart slopes nicely to the right. Then the CEO asks one question in Slack: “How much pipeline did this create?” Nobody has an answer.

I’ve sat in that silence on both sides of the table. At WiseMonk, 98% of revenue came from SEO, so I learned fast what an agency report should prove. Most guides on choosing an SEO agency are written by agencies grading their own homework. This one gives you the questions, the price benchmarks, and the red flags, plus a way to check every answer you hear.

Q1: How do you choose an SEO agency in 2026?

Choose an SEO agency in six steps. Set a revenue goal, not a ranking goal. Shortlist agencies with proof in your niche. Verify that proof in third-party traffic charts. Test whether the agency can get you cited in AI answers, not only ranked on Google. Benchmark the price against market surveys. Walk away from ranking guarantees, link quotas, and contracts with no exit.

The Six-Step Vetting Process

✅ Steps 1 to 3: Goal, Shortlist, and Proof

  1. Set a revenue goal. Write down the pipeline target, your ICP (ideal customer profile, the buyer you want), and the questions those buyers ask. “Rank for 50 keywords” is not a goal.
  2. Shortlist with niche proof. Pick three to five agencies that have grown companies like yours. Industry fit matters more than logo count.
  3. Verify the proof yourself. Ask for client domains and check their traffic in Ahrefs or Semrush from the month the agency started.

✅ Steps 4 to 6: AI Test, Price, and Exit

  1. Test AI-search ability. Ask how the agency gets brands named in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews.
  2. Benchmark the price. Compare the quote with published pricing surveys, then judge the cost of each shipped asset.
  3. Check the walk-away triggers. Google states that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google. Treat any guarantee, link quota, or 12-month lock-in as a stop sign.
When to Walk Away at Each Vetting Step
Step Walk away if...
Revenue goal The agency only talks about rankings
Niche proof They show logos but no client domains
Verified charts They refuse to share third-party data
AI-search test They can’t explain how citations are earned
Price benchmark The price hides what ships each month
Exit terms The contract has no cancellation clause

⚠️ Google-Only SEO Is Half the Job

The classic checklist assumed buyers clicked blue links. Today, many buyers read one AI answer that names a handful of brands. Gartner predicted traditional search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026. The cause, it said, is people moving to AI chatbots. An agency that only reports Google rankings is measuring the shrinking half of the market.

💡 Buyers Ask Well, Then Never Check

Most buyers already ask decent questions. The gap is verification. Phil Singleton, co-author of SEO for Growth, put it plainly:

“Asking the right questions and demanding proof is the best way to make sure that you are not hiring a risky ‘fly-by night’ service provider.”
Phil Singleton, SEO for Growth

An SEO agency should now get your brand cited inside AI answers, not just ranked as a blue link. How Agency Scorecards Used to Work

📊 The Rankings Report

Picture the monthly agency report. It shows keyword positions, impressions, and sessions, all trending up. The retainer covered a fixed scope, and the content was written to win Google’s ten blue links.

❌ What That Model Cost

Those numbers are vanity metrics, meaning they look like progress without proving revenue. Strategy often came from one firm and execution from another. Nothing in the report tied back to pipeline, so the board’s question went unanswered.

What Changed When Answers Replaced Links

⚠️ Fewer Clicks Reach Your Site

Pew Research Center tracked the browsing of 900 U.S. adults in March 2025. With a Google AI summary on the page, users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits. Without one, they clicked in 15%. Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and AI Overviews, and they get one answer naming a few brands. You are either in that answer or invisible.

💡 Rankings and Citations Are Different Games

My aha moment came when I saw brands cited by ChatGPT that weren’t in Google’s top 10. What ChatGPT trusts is not what Google trusts. Researchers from Princeton and IIT Delhi named this field GEO (Generative Engine Optimization). Their paper showed content changes can lift visibility in AI answers by up to 40%. SEO is the floor, and GEO is the building on top.

🔍 How AI Engines Pick Their Sources

Most AI engines run a live search, read the top sources, and then write one summary. That process is called retrieval. Your page has to be found, trusted, and easy to quote in short, self-contained blocks.

Third-party mentions matter just as much. When reviews, Reddit threads, and comparison listicles name your brand, the engine has outside proof that you belong in the answer.

Q3: Should you hire an agency, build in-house, or wait, and what goal should it own?

Startups that haven’t reached product-market fit rarely need a full-time SEO hire or an enterprise agency. They lack 40 hours a week of strategic SEO work and the domain authority to win competitive terms. An agency fits growth-stage companies that need strategists, writers, and developers for less than one senior hire. In-house teams win at scale, when SEO depends on product and engineering. Either way, set a pipeline goal first.

The Three Paths on the Table

🧭 Agency, In-House, or Not Yet

A founder usually lands here after a board meeting. Paid acquisition keeps getting more expensive, and someone says, “We should do SEO.” The options are an agency, an in-house hire, or waiting.

Ahrefs points to clear triggers for bringing in outside SEO help. Two examples are a steep drop in clicks and competitors getting more search traffic than you. Without a trigger like that, the spend is often premature.

Where the Experts Disagree

⚖️ The Agency Case vs the In-House Case

Phil Singleton argues an agency gives you a full team of specialists, writers, and tools. He puts the cost at roughly one junior hire. Eli Schwartz, author of Product-Led SEO, warns that early startups don’t have 40 hours of SEO work each week. He also notes that at scale, SEO needs product and engineering buy-in that outsiders struggle to drive.

“Marketing doesn’t get to tell Engineering what to do.”
A director of engineering to Eli Schwartz on his first day running SEO at SurveyMonkey, Product-Led SEO

❌ The Consulting-Only Trap

Strategy without execution rarely ships. Ethan Smith described his early consulting years this way:

“When I started with SEO consulting, I would come up with an entire strategy, all of the details, and more often than not, maybe 20% of that was launched.”
Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite

That gap is why Graphite moved to full-stack execution.

🎯 Write the Goal Brief Before You Shortlist

Before any sales call, write one page with three items:

  • ICP: who buys, and what they ask AI engines and Google.
  • BOFU queries: bottom-of-funnel questions asked close to purchase, like “best X for Y.”
  • Pipeline target: the qualified leads or revenue you expect within 12 months.

At WiseMonk, 98% of revenue came from SEO with a lean team. That taught me execution beats headcount. I might be wrong for very large companies, where internal politics slow everything down, but at growth stage, a shipping team wins.

Q4: How do you shortlist agencies and verify their proof?

Shortlist three to five agencies from peer referrals, review platforms, and agencies that rank or get cited for their own services. Ask each for three client domains. Then pull those domains’ Ahrefs or Semrush traffic charts from the month the agency started. Organic traffic should climb within three to six months. Reject logo walls and percentage claims without baselines, and call at least five live references.

Building the Shortlist

🔍 Where Good Candidates Come From

  • Peer referrals: ask founders in your category who they hired and who they fired.
  • Review platforms: read the 3-star reviews on Clutch and G2, not only the 5-star ones.
  • Their own visibility: search the agency’s core service on Google, then ask ChatGPT the same question.

An agency that is invisible for its own service is a warning sign. Singleton notes that many SEO companies “rarely even rank for their own services.”

📝 Keep the RFP Short

Send a one-page request for proposal (RFP) built from your goal brief in Q3. Ask for client domains, the named team, and a 90-day plan. Long RFPs reward agencies that write well, not agencies that ship.

The Onboarding-Month Chart Test

⭐ Ask Who They Worked With at the Start

Google advises asking a potential SEO for examples of previous work and success stories. Ethan Smith makes the test sharper:

“Ask them who they worked with when they started then look at their chart in [Ahrefs] and see if it went up.”
Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite

💡 Read Absolute Numbers, Not Percentages

A “900% traffic increase” can mean 1 visit became 10. Look for three things in each chart:

  • Absolute monthly traffic before and after onboarding
  • A steady climb that starts three to six months after onboarding
  • Growth on pages that convert, not just on blog posts

Then run the AI check. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity the buying questions a client’s customers would ask. If the agency’s clients never appear, its AI-search claims are unproven.

MaximusLabs AI’s own proof can be tested the same way. Nidra Goods ranked #1 across Google, ChatGPT, and Perplexity for “best sleep mask,” and buyers can check claims like this with a single search.

The Reference-Call Script

☎️ Call Five, Ask Four Questions

Singleton recommends speaking with at least five live client references. Ask each one:

  1. Who did the work day to day after signing?
  2. What shipped in the first 90 days?
  3. What changed in pipeline, not just in traffic?
  4. Would you hire them again at the same price?

⚠️ Watch for Scripted References

A reference who praises communication but can’t name a revenue result is telling you something. So is an agency that offers only two references, both from years ago.

MaximusLabs AI’s position is that AI citations and rankings are public, so agency proof should be checkable. MaximusLabs points buyers to results they can check, such as Oliv AI’s “64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents (Oliv AI).” A polished slide is not proof.

Q5: Which questions reveal how an agency actually works?

Ask six questions about how the work gets done. Who works on my account day to day? How do you prioritize technical fixes? How do you earn links and brand mentions? How will you get us cited in ChatGPT and Perplexity? How do you use AI in content? What do you need to know about our ICP? Good answers name people, high-impact fixes, and human-edited content.

Why Process Questions Beat Pitch Questions

💡 The Pitch Deck Hides the Process

Most sales calls cover results and price. The real risk sits in the process, meaning who does the work, what ships first, and how quality is checked. Google suggests asking any potential SEO whether they follow the Google Search Essentials, its baseline quality rules.

MaximusLabs AI’s read is that process answers are harder to fake than case studies. A rehearsed agency can polish a result slide. It struggles to explain how next Tuesday’s work gets prioritized, and that gap tells you a lot.

🎯 A Good Agency Interviews You Back

Google notes that a good SEO should ask what makes your business unique, who your competitors are, and how customers find you. If the agency asks nothing, it plans to run a template.

Q7: Which KPIs should an SEO agency be accountable for?

Hold an SEO agency to pipeline, not positions. The primary KPIs should be qualified leads, conversions, and revenue from organic search and AI referrals. Citation share across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews should back them up. Rankings prove a brand appears on Google, not that anyone bought. Fees tied to rankings or link counts reward tactics that can damage a domain.

The Ranking Report Habit

📊 A Chart That Always Goes Up

Every month, the agency sends a deck. Keyword positions climb, impressions grow, and the traffic line slopes up. The CFO asks how much revenue came from it, and the room goes quiet.

❌ Why That Report Misleads

Rankings measure presence, not demand or buying intent. Eli Schwartz, author of Product-Led SEO, compares a rankings-only report to judging paid marketing by its budget total. A big budget only proves someone can spend money.

What Happens When Nobody Tracks Revenue

⚠️ The Revenue Plateau

Schwartz describes a well-known brand whose organic revenue stalled after years of double-digit growth. Its paid team tracked every dollar of spend in detail. Its SEO team reported rankings. Traffic kept rising, but none of the work aimed at revenue, so revenue went flat.

💡 AI Answers Raise the Stakes

Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews, and they get one answer naming a few brands. Pew found users clicked traditional links in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared. Fewer clicks means traffic is a weaker proxy for revenue than ever.

A falling click-through rate can hide rising visibility. If impressions grow while clicks hold steady, your reach expanded. Track both lines separately before you call a CTR (click-through rate) drop a problem.

Eli Schwartz, Product-Led SEO

Schwartz has seen bonuses tied to links, rankings, clicks, and even revenue. Each one caused long-term problems. Pay for focused work, then judge it on pipeline over quarters.

Q8: What red flags should end the conversation?

End the conversation if an agency guarantees rankings, sells a fixed number of backlinks a month, or uses link networks. Do the same if it pitches a star strategist but won’t name your account team. A 100-page automated audit sold as strategy is another warning. So is mass-produced AI content without expert editing. Google states that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking.

The Six Red Flags

❌ Flags 1 to 3: Promises and Links

  1. Guaranteed rankings. Google warns against SEOs that guarantee rankings or claim a “special relationship” with Google. Guarantees usually target keywords nobody searches, so the promise is kept while the business gains nothing.
  2. Fixed monthly link quotas. “50 links a month” signals bought links. Google’s spam policies treat buying links for ranking purposes as a link scheme.
  3. Private blog networks. These are networks of sites built only to sell links. Google can penalize sites that use them.

❌ Flags 4 to 6: People, Audits, and Content

  1. Bait and switch. The senior strategist sells the deal, and a junior hire runs the account.
  2. The 100-page audit. Long automated reports list minor issues like missing alt text. They rarely rank fixes by revenue impact.
  3. Unedited AI content at scale. Volume without expert review weakens trust signals and E-E-A-T, Google’s measure of experience, expertise, authority, and trust.

What Practitioners Have Seen

⚠️ The Handoff

“Another red flag to look out for is being ‘handed off.’ The moment you have signed the contracts, you suddenly find yourself dealing with a new person, a junior in the company.”
Phil Singleton, SEO for Growth
“I have seen audits run on for over one hundred pages but are light on action items.”
Eli Schwartz, Product-Led SEO

Schwartz compares automated audits to using WebMD for a diagnosis. The tool lists symptoms. An experienced practitioner decides what actually matters.

💸 The Snake-Oil Dashboard

Some agencies ship dashboards that make clients feel good but move no revenue. MaximusLabs AI’s read is that this is the costliest flag, because it delays the moment anyone notices. The pattern is green charts, rising impressions, and flat pipeline. I’d rather show a client an honest flat line than a pretty one.

The New Red Flag: B-Minus Work at C-Plus Quality

⚠️ Cheap Automation Is Not Better Work

Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite, describes a new type of AI agency. It takes a “B-minus workflow,” chains ChatGPT prompts, and delivers “a C-plus, but 80% off.” Clients don’t want cheaper average content. They want better content, and retention depends on it.

✅ How to Test for It

Ask to see three recent articles and the editor’s notes on each. Ask who checked the facts and which sources they used. If nobody can answer, the workflow is automation without judgment. Walk away before the first invoice, not after the third.

Q9: Which contract terms protect you?

Agree to a 3- to 6-month initial period, then a rolling monthly retainer with 30 days’ written notice. Avoid any 12-month lock-in you can’t cancel. Name your account team in the contract. Confirm you own all content, data, and accounts. Grant only read access to Google Search Console during the audit. Tie milestones to shipped work, not to rankings.

Exit Terms Come First

⏰ The Alignment Period, Then Month to Month

SEO needs time to show results, so a short commitment is fair. Phil Singleton, co-author of SEO for Growth, recommends a 3- to 6-month initial period. After that, the deal should move to a rolling monthly retainer with a 30-day cancellation clause.

Rigid 12-month contracts with no exit or milestones shift all the risk to you. If the work stalls in month three, you keep paying for nine more months.

⚠️ Why Lock-Ins Are a Bad Sign

A lock-in replaces proof with paperwork. MaximusLabs AI’s read is simple: an agency confident in its work doesn’t need a cage.

Staffing, Ownership, and Access

✅ Name the People in Writing

Eli Schwartz, author of Product-Led SEO, warns that agencies often showcase star employees in sales, then hand the account to juniors. His fix is direct: request specific employees on your account. Put those names in the contract, plus a rule that any swap needs your written approval.

💰 Own Everything You Pay For

Your contract should state that you own every asset the agency creates. That includes:

  • All articles, pages, images, and research files
  • Analytics, Search Console, and CRM data
  • Any accounts opened on your behalf, like review profiles or tool logins

If the agency owns the accounts, leaving means starting from zero.

🔒 Limit Access During the Audit

Google advises site owners to be careful about the access they grant an SEO. During the audit, read access to Search Console is enough. Expand access only once you trust the team and the plan.

How Most First Quarters Go Wrong

📄 The 50-Page PDF

The contract is signed, and three weeks later a long audit lands in your inbox. It holds charts, tables, and a roadmap of dozens of tasks. The team starts working through it, top to bottom.

❌ The SEO Death Spiral

Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite, calls what happens next the SEO death spiral. The company then decides SEO doesn’t work, fires the agency, and repeats the cycle with someone new.

What Changed: Fewer Pages Matter More

⭐ The 5% Rule

Smith’s work points to a simple pattern. About 5% of pages and initiatives drive 85% to 95% of organic traffic and revenue. The job in the first quarter is to find that 5% and ship it, not to fix every alt tag.

Can an SEO agency guarantee rankings or AI citations?

No. Google states that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google. The same logic applies to ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini, because no agency controls how those engines choose their sources. What an agency can promise is the work itself. How do I check whether an agency can get my brand cited in ChatGPT and Perplexity?

Run the test yourself before and during the engagement. AI citations are public, so you don't need to trust a slide. Here is the simple process we recommend: • List 10 to 20 questions your buyers ask right before choosing, like "best X for Y." • Ask each question in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. • Record which brands appear and which sources each engine cites. • Repeat the same prompts monthly to track your citation share.
