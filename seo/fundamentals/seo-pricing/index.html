A VP of Marketing forwards two SEO proposals to her founder on a Friday afternoon. One is $1,200 a month. The other is $7,500. Both promise “page-one rankings,” and both attach a sample report full of keyword charts. Neither one mentions ChatGPT, Perplexity, or the AI Overview that now sits above their best-converting query. The founder replies with one line: “Which of these gets us pipeline?”

That question is the real one behind every SEO pricing search. The sections below give the actual numbers, then explain what those numbers should buy in 2027.

Q1: How Much Does SEO Cost in 2027?

In 2027, most businesses pay between $1,000 and $10,000+ a month for SEO. Agencies average about $3,209 a month, consultants about $3,250, and freelancers about $1,349, based on Ahrefs’ survey of 439 SEO providers. Basic local SEO starts near $1,000. SMB and mid-market programs usually run $2,000 to $5,000, and enterprise retainers run $6,500 to $20,000+. Most published averages trace back to one 2023 survey, so they understate budgets that must also cover AI search.

💰 Where the Averages Come From

Ahrefs found that 78.2% of SEO providers charge a monthly retainer. The most common single bracket is $501 to $1,000, yet the averages land far higher because agencies and consultants pull them up. In the United States, the median retainer sits at $1,501 to $2,000 a month.

Local SEO averages about $1,557 a month in the same survey. Phil Singleton, author of SEO for Growth, puts the floor for legitimate firms at “$2,000 to $5,000 per month, minimum.”

⚠️ Why Two Quotes Can Differ by 5x

Four factors explain most of the gap:

  1. Scope. An audit-only plan costs less than strategy, content, and technical work combined.
  2. Competition. Ranking for “CRM software” takes far more work than ranking for a local plumber.
  3. Provider type. Agencies charge roughly double what freelancers charge per month.
  4. Region. In Ahrefs’ data, 76% of India-based SEOs charge $1,000 or less a month.

The Stale-Data Problem Nobody Mentions

Almost every pricing guide on page one cites the same Ahrefs survey. That survey first ran in 2023 and was updated in 2026. Its scope was classic Google SEO. It does not price the work of getting cited inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, or Google AI Overviews.

⏰ Prices Already Moved

One 2026 industry survey reports that 70% of agencies raised prices in 2025, partly to cover AI search work. So treat any 2023 average as a floor for 2027, not a ceiling. If a quote looks “cheap” against old averages, check what it leaves out.

💸 The Number Founders Should Compare Instead

MaximusLabs AI measures every engagement on pipeline, not on mentions or rankings. That shapes how I read any SEO quote. The monthly fee is the wrong number to compare. Cost per qualified lead is the right one, and it only shows up once you ask what the retainer actually ships.

MaximusLabs AI makes this simpler by pricing work against pipeline targets agreed before the first article ships. It runs Answer Engine Optimization (AEO), the work of getting cited inside AI answers, with SEO as the foundation underneath. That way, one budget covers both Google and AI search.

Q2: How Has AI Search Changed What You Should Pay For?

AI answers now sit between buyers and your website. Pew Research Center found Google users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% without one. Semrush found the average AI search visitor is worth 4.4 times a traditional organic visitor, based on conversion rate. MaximusLabs AI took Oliv AI to a “64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents (Oliv AI).”

How SEO Budgets Used to Work

Picture the monthly SEO report. The rankings chart climbs. Organic sessions are up 18%. Then the board asks one question: where is the pipeline?

❌ What the Old Retainer Bought

For a decade, agencies sold rankings, impressions, and clicks. Those are vanity metrics, numbers that look good on a dashboard but do not show revenue. Retainers were locked to a fixed scope. Strategy often came from one firm and execution from another. Content was written for Google’s ten blue links, and nothing tied back to pipeline.

What AI Search Changed

Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Google AI Overviews. Google reports that AI Overviews has over 2.5 billion monthly users, and AI Mode has passed one billion. Pew also found users clicked a link inside the AI summary in just 1% of visits.

⚠️ The Answer Has Room for 5 to 10 Brands

An AI answer usually names 5 to 10 brands. A brand is either in that answer or invisible to the buyer. AI recommends brands it trusts, because its own credibility rides on the recommendation. Gartner predicted traditional search volume would fall 25% by 2026 as buyers move to AI assistants.

✅ Fewer Clicks, Better Clicks

The clicks that remain are worth more. Semrush’s 4.4x figure means a small stream of AI referrals can outperform a large stream of blog traffic. Being mentioned is not the finish line, though. Turning that mention into pipeline is.

Humans approve everything that ships. Most agencies only do the first pillar.

The budget stopped buying rankings and started buying answers that turn into pipeline.

MaximusLabs AI builds every 2027 plan around one test: does this spend put the brand inside the answer for the questions its buyers ask? If it does not, it does not belong in the retainer.

Q3: Which SEO Pricing Model Fits Your Stage?

Ahrefs found 78.2% of SEO providers charge monthly retainers, 48.9% charge per project, and 34.8% charge hourly. Retainers suit ongoing work. Hourly rates, most commonly $75 to $150, fit advice and audits. Project fees, most often $2,501 to $5,000, fit migrations and one-off fixes. Performance pricing tied to rankings or link counts rewards shortcuts. Value-based pricing tied to pipeline works only when attribution is clean.

The Pricing Conversation Most Founders Have

A Head of Growth sits with the CFO, comparing three proposals. The CFO asks a fair question: “Why don’t we only pay when it works?” It sounds like the safest deal on the table.

❌ How the Old Models Failed Buyers

Traditional retainers paid for activity on a fixed scope, whether or not it moved revenue. Performance deals went the other way and paid for rankings. Rankings are easy to measure but easy to game. Eli Schwartz, author of Product-Led SEO, warns that pay tied to KPIs like rankings or links “will lead toward the wrong incentives.”

What Changed When AI Search Arrived

Rankings were always a proxy. Now they are a weaker one. A page can rank first while an AI Overview answers the query above it. So a deal that pays for position three on Google may pay for a result your buyer never sees.

Link building is often a separate line item, since 62.66% of SEO agencies in Credo’s survey do not offer it at all. If a proposal is mostly reports and audits with little shipped work, the budget is paying for paperwork.

The most common padding is the audit. Eli Schwartz, author of Product-Led SEO, writes that an audit “was never meant to be just a checklist to put on a wall poster.” A $100 automated audit is like checking symptoms on WebMD. A practitioner audit is the doctor who looks at the whole patient.

MaximusLabs AI uses one test when reviewing a client’s old retainer: count what shipped. In MaximusLabs AI’s reviews, the useful number is published assets and completed fixes per month. If reports outweigh shipped work for several months running, I treat that as a warning. It is the clearest sign a budget is paying for activity, not results.

MaximusLabs AI keeps this simple by reporting shipped work and pipeline in the same monthly view. Strategy and execution sit in one team, so nothing stalls between a recommendation and a fix.

Q6: Why Is SEO Under $1,000 a Month Almost Always a Trap?

SEO under $1,000 a month rarely pays for a qualified practitioner’s time. The gap is usually filled with outsourced links, templated audits, or unedited AI-generated copy. Google’s spam policies target scaled low-value content and link schemes. AI engines also favor sources that cite evidence and read clearly. Savings at this price point often turn into cleanup costs later, so treat $1,000 a month as the practical floor.

How Cheap SEO Used to Look

A founder gets a cold email. “Page-one rankings for $499 a month.” The monthly report arrives on time, with a rankings chart and a list of 40 new backlinks. Six months later, the demo calendar looks exactly the same. Nobody can say which of those 40 links ever sent a buyer.

❌ What the $499 Plan Actually Buys

At that price, the provider cannot fund senior hours. So the work shifts to whatever scales cheaply: bulk links, thin pages, and keyword-stuffed blog posts. Phil Singleton calls the sub-$1,000 tier “where most of the snake oil is sold.” He also quotes oil-well firefighter Red Adair: “If you think it’s expensive to hire a professional to do the job, wait until you hire an amateur.”

What AI Changed About Cheap SEO

AI made content nearly free to produce. That made cheap SEO cheaper, but not better. Graphite CEO Ethan Smith describes the pattern as taking a “B-minus” workflow, chaining ChatGPT prompts, and getting “a C-plus, but 80% off.”

⚠️ Why Cheap Content Fails Twice Now

The output now fails on two fronts:

  • Google: Its spam policies call out “scaled content abuse,” meaning many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings, plus link spam.
  • AI engines: The KDD 2024 GEO study found keyword stuffing gave little to no visibility gain in generative engines. Citing sources and adding statistics helped instead.

So a cheap plan can lose Google rankings and still miss AI answers. A brand that is absent from the answer is invisible to the buyer who asked.

What MaximusLabs AI Does Instead

MaximusLabs AI uses AI where it is strong: research, source mapping, and formatting. Senior humans write in the founder’s voice and approve everything that ships. That is how AI lowers cost without lowering trust.

💸 The Cleanup Bill Nobody Quotes

MaximusLabs AI opens every engagement with an in-depth technical audit. When a low-cost provider came before, that audit is where thin pages, risky links, and rewrite candidates tend to surface. My view is simple: AI makes content cheap, but it does not make it trustworthy. Trust is what gets a brand cited, and citations are what lead to pipeline.

✅ The Floor Rule

Use $1,000 a month as the floor for basic local SEO. Use $2,000 to $5,000 as the floor for B2B SaaS programs that need content, technical work, and AI citations together. Below those numbers, ask exactly who does the work and how many senior hours you get.

MaximusLabs AI measures every engagement on pipeline, not on link counts or rankings. That keeps cheap shortcuts off the table, because shortcuts rarely survive a pipeline review.

So a separate “AI Overview fee” deserves scrutiny. SEO and AEO work best as one program.

Pick the cheapest tool that covers your prompts and engines. Major SEO suites are also adding AI-visibility tracking to existing plans.

✅ Google’s Own Guidance

Google’s Search Central documentation says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Pages must be indexed and eligible for a snippet. That means strong SEO is the base layer for AI visibility, not a separate project.

One team optimizes pages for Google while the other chases citations with different priorities.

MaximusLabs AI runs AEO as the flagship service, with SEO as the foundation, in one strategy-and-execution team. That keeps one plan, one source map, and one pipeline scorecard.

Q8: How Do You Measure SEO ROI, and How Long Until It Pays Back?

SEO and AEO return on investment (ROI) is best measured on demo requests, qualified leads, and pipeline, including AI-referral traffic tracked in Google Analytics 4 (GA4). Rankings alone hide whether revenue moved. Organic content keeps working after it ships, unlike paid traffic, which stops when spend stops. MaximusLabs AI starts engagements with bottom-of-funnel content and reports pipeline first. For Oliv AI, that meant “27 qualified leads, $47K+ pipeline, 26% close rate in 4 months (Oliv AI).”

How SEO ROI Used to Be Reported

The quarterly business review opens with a slide. Keyword rankings are up 40%. Organic sessions are up 22%. The CFO asks how much pipeline that created, and the room goes quiet.

❌ Rankings Without Revenue

Eli Schwartz saw this at SurveyMonkey. Leadership tracked paid attribution in fine detail but judged SEO only by rankings. When organic revenue stalled, rankings were still rising. Once the team measured lead forms and demo requests instead, it found ranking gains were not producing revenue.

What Changed With AI Search

AI search makes ranking reports even less useful. Google says traffic from AI Overviews and AI Mode appears inside overall search data in Search Console. So the AI share is hard to see on its own. Meanwhile, buyers who arrive from ChatGPT or Perplexity are often further along, which is why Semrush values them at 4.4 times an organic visitor.

💰 Renting vs Owning

HubSpot co-founder Dharmesh Shah compares paid ads to renting someone else’s stage. Turn off spend, and the traffic stops. Schwartz compares SEO to owning a house, where each month adds a little equity. That is why SEO payback is slower at the start but compounds later.

📊 What to Track Instead

  1. Qualified leads and demo requests from organic and AI sources.
  2. Pipeline influenced by pages buyers visited before converting.
  3. AI referrals using a custom channel group in GA4 for sources like chatgpt.com and perplexity.ai. ChatGPT began adding a source tag to many outbound links in 2025.
  4. AI citation rate for the buying questions your ICP asks.

Q9: How Do You Vet an SEO Proposal Before Signing?

Vet an SEO proposal on proof and contract terms, not on slides. Ask for five live client references, plus third-party traffic charts that start from each client’s onboarding month. Request a 3- to 6-month trial followed by a 30-day exit clause, since only about 30% of agencies in Credo’s survey set no minimum term. Reject audits with no priority scoring. Confirm the provider ships the work, not just the recommendations.

The Death Spiral This Checklist Prevents

A Head of Growth signs a 12-month SEO contract. Month one brings a 50-page audit PDF. By month six, the team has fixed alt tags and H1 tags, the main heading on each page, and pipeline has not moved.

⚠️ How the Spiral Works

Graphite CEO Ethan Smith calls this a “death spiral of zero impact work.” The agency asks for patience, leadership loses trust, and the agency gets fired. Then the cycle starts again with a new vendor. Smith has also said that when he worked as a strategy-only consultant, clients launched only about 20% of what he recommended.

Step 1: Ask for Proof You Can Check

✅ References and Traffic Charts

  1. Five live client references. Talk to current clients, not just the ones on the website.
  2. Third-party traffic charts. Ask for Ahrefs or Semrush organic charts that start from the month each client signed. A curve that was already rising before onboarding proves little.
  3. AI citation evidence. Ask which buying questions each client now appears for in ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews.

Red flag: The provider only shares screenshots or anonymized results, with no client you can call.

Step 2: Check the Contract Terms

⏰ Trial Period and Exit Clause

In Credo’s survey, about 30% of agencies asked for a minimum of 6 to 12 months. Another 40% asked for one to six months. A fair structure is a 3- to 6-month alignment period, then a 30-day written notice to exit.

Red flag: A 12-month lock-in with no exit, signed before any work ships.

Is $500 a month enough to pay for SEO in 2027?

In most cases, no. At $500 a month, a provider cannot fund senior practitioner time, so the work shifts to whatever scales cheaply: bulk links, thin pages, and unedited AI copy. We treat $1,000 a month as the floor for basic local SEO and $2,000 to $5,000 as the floor for B2B SaaS programs. Cheap plans now fail twice: • Google: its spam policies target scaled low-value content and link schemes. • AI engines: ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini favor sources that cite evidence and read clearly, so thin content rarely gets cited. MaximusLabs AI opens every engagement with an in-depth technical audit, and when a low-cost provider came before, that audit is usually where thin pages, risky links, and rewrite candidates surface. The savings from a $500 plan often turn into cleanup costs later. If your budget is tight, we suggest a smaller, well-scoped project, such as an audit plus a few bottom-of-funnel pages, instead of a cheap monthly retainer. Ask any low-cost provider exactly who does the work and how many senior hours you get each month.

How long does SEO take to pay back its cost?

SEO payback is slower at the start than paid ads, but it compounds, because organic pages keep working after they ship. Paid traffic stops the day spend stops. We define payback as the first quarter in which pipeline from SEO and AI search covers the program's cost. Three things shorten that timeline: • Starting with bottom-of-funnel pages, such as comparison and alternatives pages, so early wins show up as demos, not page views. • Tracking AI referrals from ChatGPT and Perplexity separately in GA4, since those visitors often arrive further along. • Measuring pipeline, not rankings, so you can see real payback instead of guessing. Why do two SEO agencies quote such different prices for the same work?

Two quotes for "the same work" rarely cover the same work. Ahrefs' survey of 439 providers found agencies average about $3,209 a month while freelancers average about $1,349, and the gap widens further with scope and competition. Four factors explain most of the difference: • Scope: an audit-only plan costs less than strategy, content, and technical work combined. • Competition: ranking for a crowded SaaS category takes far more work than a local service term. • Provider type: agencies charge roughly double what freelancers charge per month. • What actually ships: some retainers are mostly reports, while others publish pages and complete fixes. We suggest asking each provider to split the fee into strategy, content, technical work, and tools, then count how many assets ship each month. MaximusLabs AI uses that same test when reviewing a client's old retainer: count what shipped. The better comparison is cost per qualified lead, not the monthly fee. Is pay-for-performance SEO a good deal?

Rarely. Paying only for rankings sounds safe, but rankings are easy to measure and easy to game. Pay tied to positions or link counts can reward shortcuts that later trigger penalties. SE Ranking's pricing survey found only 6% of agencies use pay-for-performance pricing, usually mixed with other models. AI search makes ranking-based deals weaker still. What should a monthly SEO package include?

A monthly SEO package should cover four buckets, each visible in the proposal: • Strategy: research, priorities, and reporting, with a stated number of senior hours. • Content: briefs, writing, editing, and design, with a stated number of assets shipped each month. • Technical work: crawl fixes, schema, speed, and site structure, with clarity on who makes each change. • Tools: SEO suites and tracking software, which typically cost $100 to $500 a month. Watch for three hidden gaps. Link building is often billed separately. "Content strategy" may mean a plan, not published articles. "Technical recommendations" may mean a list for your developer, not completed fixes.
