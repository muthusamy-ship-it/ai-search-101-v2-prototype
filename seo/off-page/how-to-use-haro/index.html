Q1. Is HARO still worth using in 2026, now that AI engines pick the answer?

Yes. Cision shut down Connectively, the 2024 rebrand of HARO, on December 9, 2024. Featured.com bought HARO and restarted its free email digests on April 22, 2025. HARO matters more now because AI engines cite earned media. Muck Rack found earned sources drive 84% of AI citations, while paid content drives 0.3%. MaximusLabs AI applies this in client work. Oliv AI reached a 64% AI citation rate in 6 months, vs ~30% for billion-dollar incumbents.

The Old Link Playbook Is Breaking

📊 The Monthly Report Nobody Trusts

A Head of Growth opens the monthly SEO report. Referring domains went up again, and the backlink chart points north. Then the CEO asks one question: why does ChatGPT recommend three competitors and not us?

I hear that question from founders constantly. The report measures links, but buyers now ask AI engines.

❌ What Link Begging Actually Cost

Traditional SEO agencies built links by asking for them. Ethan Smith, CEO of Graphite, describes the routine plainly: email site owners and beg for a link. Agencies reported it as Domain Rating (a 0 to 100 site authority score).

Nothing in that report tied back to pipeline. It optimized one engine while buyers moved to five.

What Changed When AI Started Answering

🔍 Mentions Now Beat Links

Ahrefs studied 75,000 brands in 2025. Brand web mentions, linked or unlinked, showed a 0.664 correlation with AI Overview visibility. Backlinks showed just 0.218.

Muck Rack's analysis of over 25 million AI-cited links points the same way:

⭐ Why a HARO Quote Is a Trust Transfer

Here's the thing. When ChatGPT names your brand, it stakes its own credibility on you. Buyers see five to ten brands in one answer, so you're either in or invisible.

A HARO quote puts your name inside the journalism AI engines already trust. As Eli Schwartz puts it in Product-Led SEO, "Brands don't build links; they get links." HARO is one of the cleanest ways to get them.

Q2. How do you use HARO step by step, starting with a profile journalists trust?

Using HARO takes seven steps:

  1. Sign up free as a source at helpareporter.com.
  2. Verify LinkedIn, and use one consistent name and title everywhere.
  3. Filter the three daily digests for queries your buyers would read.
  4. Reply within a few hours.
  5. Lead with a quotable answer under 250 words.
  6. Close with a two-line credential, and never ask for a link.
  7. Track placements and AI citations, not just link counts.

Step 1: Create Your Source Account

📝 Signing Up Takes Five Minutes

Go to helpareporter.com and register as a source, meaning someone who answers journalist requests. HARO stays free for both journalists and sources, funded by newsletter sponsors. You will start receiving query digests by email in the morning, afternoon, and evening.

Pick categories that match your real expertise. A narrow set of categories keeps your inbox readable and your replies relevant.

⚠️ Read the Rules Before Your First Pitch

Every query lists the outlet type, the deadline, and the requirements. Some ask for a specific job title or years of experience. If you don't meet them, skip the query.

Ignoring requirements is the fastest way to look like spam. Journalists notice, and they remember names. A skipped query costs nothing, while a careless reply can cost you that reporter for good.

Step 2: Pass Verification and Vetting

🔒 How Featured Screens Sources Now

Featured rebuilt HARO's vetting after the Connectively years. It combines AI signals, AI headshot detection, behavioral patterns, human review, LinkedIn verification, and community reporting. Since September 2025, HARO has also run Pangram AI detection on submitted pitches.

That means a thin profile hurts you before your pitch is read. Complete your LinkedIn with a real photo, your current role, and a clear headline.

🧾 What a Strong Profile Includes

  • A real headshot (no AI-generated images)
  • Your exact job title and company
  • Two or three lines of relevant experience
  • A link to an author page on your own site

Journalists check sources in seconds. Make that check easy.

Step 3: Lock In One Consistent Identity

🪪 Same Name, Every Surface

Eric Enge's rule in The Art of SEO is simple. Use the exact same byline name on all content and social profiles. That consistency helps search engines gain confidence about who you are.

The same logic applies to AI engines. LLMs (large language models, the systems behind ChatGPT and Gemini) connect mentions to an entity. If you are "Jon Smith" on HARO and "Jonathan R. Smith" on LinkedIn, those mentions may never connect.

✍️ Write a Bio Line You Can Reuse

Draft one credential line and reuse it in every pitch. Here is an illustrative version:

Priya Rao, Head of RevOps, 200-person B2B SaaS company, 9 years in RevOps.

It states role, context, and experience in one breath.

I think this is the most underrated step. When I audit why a founder's HARO quotes never show up in AI answers, inconsistent names are often part of the reason.

MaximusLabs AI sets up this identity layer before any outreach starts. Bylines, author pages, and LinkedIn profiles are aligned first. That way, every placement adds to one recognizable entity that both journalists and AI engines can verify.

Q3. Which HARO queries should you answer, and which should you skip?

Answer queries where you have first-hand experience and where the outlet's readers look like your buyers. Skip queries outside your expertise, anonymous requests, and mass roundups that attract hundreds of replies. Niche trade publications often beat famous mastheads. Selection rates tend to be higher, and the link is topically relevant. That specific article URL is also likelier to surface in AI answers for your category.

Why High Domain Rating Is the Wrong Filter

📉 The Vanity Trap

Most teams sort HARO queries by Domain Rating and chase the biggest names. A DR 90 lifestyle site feels like a win. But if its readers will never buy your product, the placement moves nothing in your pipeline.

A revenue-first filter asks a different question. Would my ICP (ideal customer profile, the buyer you actually sell to) read this article?

🎯 What a Good Query Looks Like

Score each query against four checks before you write a word:

Four Checks Before Answering a HARO Query
Check Answer if yes Skip if no
First-hand experience You have done this work yourself You'd be guessing or summarizing
Reader fit The outlet's audience matches your ICP The audience will never buy from you
Clear outlet The publication is named or verifiable Anonymous outlet, vague request
Low crowding Niche or technical question Generic roundup ("share your best tip")

Three yes answers is my bar. Fewer than three, and I move on.

Think at the URL Level, Not the Domain Level

🔗 Why the Specific Article Matters

Ethan Smith makes a sharp point about AI citations. It's not that you're mentioned by a big site, "it's that you're mentioned on this specific URL." AI engines cite individual pages that match a question, not whole domains.

So picture the article the journalist will publish. Will it answer a question your buyers ask ChatGPT? If yes, that one page can carry your name into AI answers for months.

🧭 Niche Beats Famous

A quote in a focused SaaS operations newsletter often outperforms a line in a national roundup. The topic match is tighter, competition is lower, and the page stays relevant longer.

Sam Oh, VP of Marketing at Ahrefs, credits his HARO wins to speed, evident experience, and offering to answer follow-ups. Evident experience only shows up when the query fits your work.

Set Up Filters So You Don't Drown

📬 Build an Inbox That Works for You

HARO sends three digests a day, and most queries won't fit. Set up email filters before the volume buries you:

  1. Create a folder for HARO digests.
  2. Add keyword filters for your core topics (for example, "RevOps," "SaaS pricing," "sales forecasting").
  3. Route matches to a priority label with a mobile notification.
  4. Review the priority label within an hour of each send.

⏱️ Give Each Query a 60-Second Decision

Read the query, run the four checks, and decide. Anything that takes longer than a minute to justify is usually a skip. Saying no fast is what frees up time to say yes well.

MaximusLabs AI ranks HARO queries by ICP fit and by the buyer prompts each article could influence. Domain Rating is a tiebreaker, not the starting point. That keeps the effort pointed at pipeline instead of at a prettier backlink chart.

Q4. How do you write a HARO pitch that actually gets picked?

Answer the journalist's exact question in your first two sentences, so they can quote it as is. Add one specific number or first-hand example. Then add a two-line credential. Keep the pitch under 250 words. Muck Rack's 2025 data favors one-to-one pitches under 300 words. Never ask for a link or anchor text, because journalists treat link-seekers as spammers.

The Anatomy of a Pitch That Gets Quoted

🧱 Four Parts, In This Order

  1. The quotable answer: one or two sentences that directly answer the question.
  2. The proof: a number, a result, or a short story from your own work.
  3. The context: one or two lines on why this matters to the reader.
  4. The credential: your name, title, company, and relevant experience.

Journalists skim dozens of replies per query. If your first two sentences can't be pasted into the article, the rest rarely gets read.

❌ What to Leave Out

  • Company boilerplate and product features
  • Attachments (use links instead)
  • Requests for a link, a dofollow tag, or specific anchor text
  • Generic advice anyone could give

Weak Pitch vs. Strong Pitch

⚖️ The Same Expert, Two Outcomes

Illustrative query: "How are B2B sales teams improving forecast accuracy?" (The examples below are hypothetical, built to show structure.)

Weak Pitch vs. Strong Pitch (Illustrative)
Weak pitch Strong pitch
"Our AI platform leads the forecasting category..." "Teams improve accuracy by forecasting from deal activity, not rep opinion."
Lists five product features Shares one result: "We cut our forecast miss from 22% to 9% in two quarters."
Ends with "Please link to our homepage" Ends with a two-line credential and an offer to answer follow-ups
450 words 180 words

The strong pitch reads like journalism. The weak one reads like an ad.

🏢 The Denver Post Turnaround

In The Art of SEO, Stephan Spencer describes a client, GHC Housing, renovating a Denver apartment building. The first draft to a journalist read like a mini press release. Stephan reframed it as expert commentary on a recent Denver Post article about rising rents.

Within hours, the journalist passed it to the affordable-housing beat reporter. The result was a full-page feature and an important deal for the client. The link mattered less than the coverage.

Why Asking for the Link Backfires

🚫 The Link-Seeking Mindset

Enge and Spencer put it bluntly: "try to get out of the link-seeking mindset." Asking for a link makes the pitch about you, not their readers. Journalists then file you as a spammer.

Many outlets link to sources anyway when the quote is strong. If they don't, the mention still counts with AI engines. Brand mentions, linked or unlinked, were the strongest AI Overview signal in the Ahrefs study.

🚂 Ask With Value, Not Permission

Years ago, I walked into a Southern Railways office with zero experience and asked about renting a train. I had no credentials, just a clear ask and a reason it was worth their time. HARO pitching works the same way. You don't need permission to pitch, only something worth quoting.

MaximusLabs AI drafts pitches from the founder's own words, gathered in interviews. Every pitch ships with a real number from the client's work and a human review before it goes out.

Q5. How fast should you respond, and when should you follow up?

Reply within a few hours of a HARO digest landing. Journalists often stop reading replies well before the stated deadline. Send at most two follow-ups, and only when each adds something useful. That might be a data point, a call offer, or an answer to their question. After two follow-ups, most reporters stop opening your emails, and that costs you future stories.

Why Speed Wins More Placements Than Polish

⏰ The Deadline Is Not the Real Deadline

A query might say "deadline: Friday 5 PM." In practice, many journalists start writing as soon as a few strong answers arrive. Once the quote slots are filled, later replies often go unread.

Sam Oh, VP of Marketing at Ahrefs, lists speed to response as the first factor behind his HARO wins. Evident experience and answering follow-ups come next. A good pitch sent in two hours beats a perfect pitch sent in two days.

🔦 Headlights at Night

I think about HARO the way I think about driving at night. Your headlights only show the next 100 meters, and that's enough to keep moving. You don't need a perfect pitch, just a clear, useful one sent now.

That doesn't mean sloppy. It means you prepare the parts that never change, so only the answer is new each time.

Build a Daily Digest Routine

📅 A Routine That Fits a Real Workday

HARO sends three digests a day: morning, afternoon, and evening. Here is a routine that keeps response times short without eating your day:

A Daily HARO Digest Routine
Step What you do Time
1. Scan Check your priority label after each digest 5 minutes
2. Decide Run the four-check filter from Q3 1 minute per query
3. Draft Write the quotable answer and proof 15 to 20 minutes
4. Send Paste your saved credential line and send 2 minutes

✅ Prepare What Doesn't Change

Keep a short file of reusable parts. That way, each pitch only needs the answer itself.

  • Your credential line (from Q2)
  • Three or four proof points with real numbers
  • A headshot link and an author page link
  • Your phone number and the hours you can take a call

How to Follow Up Without Burning the Relationship

🔁 The Two Follow-Up Rule

Send no more than two follow-ups per pitch. Each one must add value, not just ask "did you see my email?"

  1. First follow-up (1 to 2 days later): offer a new data point or a short extra example.
  2. Second follow-up (about a week later): offer a quick call, or answer an open question from the query.

Muck Rack's 2025 creator journalism survey found 82% of creator journalists want any follow-up within the first week. After that, the story has usually moved on.

⚠️ When to Stop

If there's no reply after two follow-ups, stop. Silence usually means the story filled up, not that your pitch was bad. Pushing harder turns a neutral contact into one who remembers you as a pest.

Instead, note the journalist's beat in a simple spreadsheet. When their next query fits, you'll pitch with context. That second pitch often lands better than the first.

MaximusLabs AI runs this routine as a shared queue, with priority alerts on ICP-fit queries. Credential lines and proof points are kept ready, so client pitches go out within hours, not days.

Q6. Can you use AI for HARO pitches without getting flagged?

Use AI for research, not for writing the pitch. HARO runs Pangram AI detection on every pitch, and journalists can see the score. Across Featured's platforms, about 1 in 10 fully AI-generated pitches were selected, versus about 1 in 3 human pitches. Banned accounts were 3.6x more likely to have sent a fully AI-generated pitch. MaximusLabs AI uses AI for research and structure, while humans write each pitch in the expert's own voice.

The Shortcut Everyone Is Taking

🤖 The 40-Pitch Morning

A marketing manager opens the morning HARO digest. There are forty queries, and the CMO wants more press. So the manager pastes each query into ChatGPT, tweaks the name, and hits send forty times.

It feels efficient. The dashboard shows forty pitches sent. But the placement count stays flat for weeks.

❌ Why Volume Became the Problem

Traditional link-building agencies sold pitch volume. More replies meant more chances, and HARO's late years filled with templated spam. Featured CEO Brett Farmiloe says AI has made media pitching noisier and less effective.

Volume was always a vanity metric. Nothing about forty sends ties back to pipeline.

⭐ Why AI Engines Care Too

AI search engines cite journalism because it carries real expertise. A quote that reads like ChatGPT output adds nothing new to that pool. Everyone summarizing five articles to write a sixth is exactly the problem.

A real perspective is the moat. It wins the journalist first, then the AI answer that later cites the article.

MaximusLabs AI captures the founder's perspective first, then uses AI only for research and structure. Every pitch reads as the expert, because the expert's thinking is in it.

Q7. What should you do once your quote goes live?

Thank the journalist and share the article. If the link is nofollow, accept it. Asking for a dofollow link risks having it removed, and Google treats rel attributes as hints. If you're quoted without a link, a polite reclamation email usually works. AI engines read the mention either way. Add every placement to a press page on your site so the trust signals compound over time.

Close the Loop With the Journalist

🙏 Say Thanks the Same Day

Send a two-line thank-you once the piece is live. Mention one thing you liked about the article. Then offer to help with future stories on your topic.

This small step turns a one-time source into a repeat one. Journalists keep lists of people who answer fast and quote well.

📣 Share It Where Your Buyers Are

Share the article on LinkedIn, tag the journalist, and add a sentence on why the topic matters. Send it to your sales team too. A third-party quote often works better in a sales email than your own claims.

Handle Nofollow Links the Right Way

🔗 What Nofollow Actually Means

A nofollow link carries a rel="nofollow" tag in its code. It tells Google not to associate the linking site with the linked page. Google also offers rel="sponsored" for paid links and rel="ugc" for user-generated content.

Since 2019, Google has treated these attributes as hints, not strict commands. A nofollow link from a trusted outlet isn't worthless, even for classic SEO.

⚠️ Never Ask for a Dofollow Switch

Asking an editor to change nofollow to dofollow makes you look like a link-seeker. Some editors respond by removing the link entirely. Accept what you get, and thank them.

Obsessing over dofollow is 2019 thinking. For AI search, the mention is what counts. The Ahrefs study found brand mentions, linked or unlinked, correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664.

Reclaim Unlinked Mentions

🔍 Find Quotes Without Links

Sometimes your quote runs with your name but no link. Eric Enge calls unlinked mention reclamation one of the easiest, cheapest, and most effective link-building tactics.

Set up alerts for your name and company name. Check each new mention for a missing link.

✉️ Send a Short, Polite Note

Keep the reclamation email under 60 words. Short notes get read, and long ones feel like a demand:

  1. Thank them for including your quote.
  2. Ask if they'd add a link to your author page or relevant resource.
  3. Give the exact URL, and make it easy to say yes.

If they decline, let it go. The mention still feeds AI engines, and the relationship matters more than one link.

Make the Placement Work Harder

🏛️ Build a Press Page

List every placement on a dedicated press or "as featured in" page. Link to each article, and add the date and outlet name. This gives journalists, buyers, and AI crawlers one place to confirm your expertise.

MaximusLabs AI logs every placement with its link type, then tracks whether the article starts appearing in AI answers. Mentions that get cited are studied, so future pitches target similar outlets.

Q8. How do you measure HARO ROI beyond counting backlinks?

Track three layers. First, placements won, with follow and nofollow links counted separately. Second, AI citation share for buyer prompts. Third, pipeline influenced by the coverage. Counting links alone is a habit from Google-only SEO. MaximusLabs AI measures citation share across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google. Nidra Goods ranked #1 across all three for "best sleep mask" from a single GEO strategy.

Why Link Counts Mislead

📉 The Chart That Goes Up and Means Nothing

Many teams report HARO results as "12 new backlinks this quarter." That number says nothing about whether buyers saw the coverage. It also says nothing about whether AI engines now recommend you.

Revenue is the only metric that pays the bills. Clicks, impressions, and link counts are inputs at best. A placement that never reaches a buyer, or an AI answer, is a cost, not a win.

📈 The Industry Is Already Shifting

Muck Rack's 2025 PR measurement report found 78% of respondents say tracking AI-generated mentions matters to their work. And 67% expect visibility in LLMs to become a standard metric.

The Three-Layer Measurement Stack

🧱 Layer by Layer

The Three-Layer HARO Measurement Stack
Layer What to track How to track it
1. Placements Articles won, follow vs nofollow, outlet fit Spreadsheet log, Google Search Console links report
2. AI citation share How often AI names your brand for buyer prompts Fixed prompt set, run weekly across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI Overviews
3. Pipeline Leads and deals touched by coverage or AI answers CRM source field, "How did you hear about us?" with AI options, AI referral traffic

Layer 1 is the easiest to measure and the weakest signal. Layer 3 is the hardest and the one your CFO cares about.

🔎 How to Run the Prompt Set

  1. Write 20 to 30 questions your buyers would ask an AI engine.
  2. Run each one weekly in every major engine.
  3. Record whether your brand is named and which URL is cited.
  4. Note when a HARO article starts appearing as a cited source.

That last step connects a single placement to AI visibility. Keep the prompt set fixed for at least a quarter, so week-to-week changes mean something.

A Quarterly Cadence That Works

📅 Set a Realistic Target

Aim for two to four media appearances per quarter, across HARO, podcasts, and guest commentary. That pace builds steady mentions without burning out your expert.

Review all three layers at the end of each quarter. Cut outlet types that never show up in AI answers or pipeline. Double down on the ones that do, even if their Domain Rating looks modest.

Q9. Should you also use Qwoted, Source of Sources, or Featured?

Yes, if HARO alone isn't producing placements. Source of Sources, launched by HARO founder Peter Shankman, is a free email digest of journalist requests. Qwoted is a platform where journalists post source requests and experts pitch using credits. Featured, which also owns HARO, runs a structured question-and-answer format. Most B2B teams do best on two platforms, chosen by goal: link volume, publication quality, or predictable placements.

Why One Platform Is No Longer Enough

🧩 The Market Split in 2024

For years, HARO was the default. Then Cision rebranded it as Connectively, added paid tiers, and shut it down in December 2024. Journalists and sources scattered while the platform was down.

Some journalists went to Qwoted. Some followed Peter Shankman to Source of Sources. Others stayed with Featured, which later revived HARO itself. The result is simple: the journalist your buyers read might only post on one of them.

🌐 Be Where Journalists Look

I think of this as Search Everywhere Optimization. AI engines pull from many sources, and journalists source from many platforms. Being present on only one means missing stories you were qualified to win.

❌ Why the Old Fallback Fails

The traditional fallback was the press release. Agencies pushed releases through wire services, packed with keyword-rich links back to the site. Eric Enge calls that a form of voting for yourself.

Wire links rarely move rankings, and AI engines largely skip them. Muck Rack found press releases are cited far less often than journalism, though they can seed stories reporters later write.

What Changed: Journalists and AI Both Want Original Data

📊 Data Is the Pitch

Journalists need fresh facts they can't find elsewhere. AI engines lean on the journalism that reports those facts. Earned media drives about 84% of AI citations, while paid content drives 0.3%.

So one original dataset can feed both. It earns the article, and then the article earns the AI citation. Ethan Smith describes this exact path: Graphite published an AI content study, and Axios wrote about it.

How to Build Your Own Story

🛠️ A Proactive Pitch Playbook

  1. Run one small study. Survey 200 to 500 people in your ICP, or analyze your own anonymized product data.
  2. Find the surprising stat. Journalists want the number nobody expected.
  3. Watch your target beats. Look for recent articles your data could extend or challenge.
  4. Find the contact fast. I give myself one minute to find a journalist's email, then move on.
  5. Pitch the angle, not the brand. Lead with the finding, and offer the full dataset.

Is HARO free to use in 2026?

Yes. HARO is free for both journalists and sources in 2026. Featured.com bought HARO from Cision in April 2025, restarted the classic email digests on April 22, 2025, and funds the service through newsletter sponsorships instead of source fees. Here is what you get at no cost: • Up to three query digests a day (morning, afternoon, and evening) • Direct email replies to journalists, with no pitch credits • Access to queries across many categories The real cost is time. We budget roughly 20 to 30 minutes per pitch once the reusable parts (credential line, proof points, headshot link) are ready. Without that prep, HARO quietly eats an afternoon a day. Paid tiers exist on related platforms, such as Qwoted and Featured's own Q&A product, but you don't need them to start. Are HARO backlinks dofollow or nofollow?

Both. Each publication sets its own link policy, so a HARO placement can carry a dofollow link, a nofollow link, or no link at all. Many large news sites mark outbound links as nofollow by default. We don't treat nofollow as a loss, for three reasons: • Since 2019, Google has treated rel attributes like nofollow as hints, not strict commands. • The Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands found brand mentions, linked or unlinked, correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664, versus 0.218 for backlinks. • AI engines read the mention and the surrounding context, regardless of the link tag. What we never do is ask an editor to switch a nofollow link to dofollow. It makes you look like a link-seeker, and some editors remove the link entirely. If a quote runs with no link, a short, polite reclamation note usually works. MaximusLabs AI logs every placement with its link type, then tracks whether the article starts appearing in AI answers. You can see how we track that in our breakdown of brand mention tracking tools.

Do HARO mentions help you show up in ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews?

They can, because AI engines lean heavily on earned media. Muck Rack's May 2026 analysis of over 25 million AI-cited links found earned sources drive about 84% of citations, while paid content drives 0.3%. Journalism alone accounts for about 27%. A HARO quote puts your name inside that journalism. When ChatGPT or an AI Overview later cites the article, your brand comes with it. Three things decide whether a placement actually helps: • Topic fit: the article must answer a question your buyers ask AI engines. • Name consistency: the same name and title everywhere, so engines connect mentions to one entity. • Volume over time: one quote rarely moves AI visibility on its own. How long does it take to get your first HARO placement?

There is no fixed timeline, and anyone promising one is guessing. Placement speed depends on your niche, how many queries fit your expertise, and how fast you reply. In our experience, three habits shorten the wait more than anything else: • Speed: reply within a few hours of a digest landing, since journalists often stop reading long before the deadline. • Selectivity: answer only queries where you have first-hand experience and the outlet's readers match your buyers. • Quotability: lead with an answer the journalist can paste straight into the article. Expect some silent weeks. Silence usually means the story filled up, not that your pitch was bad. We track the journalist's beat and pitch again when the next query fits, which often lands better than the first attempt. How many HARO pitches should you send each week?

Fewer than most people think. We would rather send three well-fitted pitches than thirty generic ones. Volume without fit is a vanity metric, and it trains journalists to ignore your name. The data backs this up. Pangram scored 1,394,740 pitches across Featured, HARO, and Connectively between June 2025 and July 2026. About 1 in 10 fully AI-generated pitches were selected, versus about 1 in 3 human pitches. Mass-produced replies simply don't win. Instead of a weekly quota, we use a filter. Answer a query only if it passes at least three of these four checks: • You have done this work yourself. • The outlet's audience matches your ideal customer profile. • The publication is named or verifiable. • It is a niche or technical question, not a generic roundup. That filter usually leaves a handful of strong queries each week, which is plenty. Why are my HARO pitches never getting picked?

Usually it comes down to a few fixable patterns. When we review pitches that keep missing, we see the same issues again and again: • The answer is buried. The first two sentences are context or company background, not a quotable answer. • The pitch is too long. Muck Rack's 2025 data favors one-to-one pitches under 300 words, and we aim for under 250. • It reads like an ad. Product features and boilerplate signal promotion, not expertise. • It asks for a link. Journalists treat link requests as spam. • It sounds AI-written. HARO runs AI detection on every pitch, and journalists can see the score. • It arrives late. Quote slots often fill hours after a digest lands. Fix the order first. Lead with the answer, add one specific number or first-hand example, then close with a two-line credential. Also check your profile. A thin LinkedIn or an inconsistent name can lose the pitch before it is read. Should you handle HARO in-house or hire an agency?

It depends on what you want HARO to do. If you just want a few backlinks, an in-house marketer with a filter and a pitch routine can run it well. The process in this guide needs no special tools. An outside partner makes more sense when you need HARO to drive AI visibility and pipeline, not just links. That requires work most teams don't have time for: • Mapping which outlets each AI engine actually trusts in your category • Tracking whether placements start appearing in AI answers • Writing pitches that sound like your founder, not like a template • Tying coverage back to qualified leads Be careful with link-building agencies that sell pitch volume or guaranteed links. Mass pitching now performs poorly, and a pitch that reads like a template can cost you the journalist.
